\begin{abstract}
We document the BFS unstable-particle EFT cross-section chain implemented
for the FCC-ee $W$-pair threshold scan. The chain combines the full Born
expansion through $N^{3/2}\text{LO}_\text{EFT}$, the NLO loop corrections
(hard$+$soft$+$collinear, NLO Coulomb, EW decay), the dominant NNLO
$\sigma^{(3/2)}_\text{LR}$ contributions of eq.~(49) of \cite{BFSnnlo},
a multiplicative QCD width correction $\delta_\text{QCD}$, a Whizard
exact-Born anchor on the Born sum, and an eMELA next-to-leading-log
(NLL) ISR convolution (DELTA factorisation, $\alpha(M_Z)$
renormalisation; the $\mathrm{LL{+}exp}$ BETA-scheme radiator is retained
only for the BFS-table closure reruns). Every numerical table in this report
corresponds to a script in the repository (cf.~\code{report/README.md});
all numbers shown are reproduced verbatim from the current code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Goal and scope}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The FCC-ee threshold scan aims at $\sim$MeV-precision $\mW$ from
$e^+e^-\to W^+W^- \to$ 4 fermions in the energy window
$\sqrts\in[157,163]\GeV$. With $\sim 10^6$ events per scan point this
requires a theory prediction whose residual uncertainty on
$\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\mW$ is well below $0.1\%/\!\MeV$. The
prediction is built and validated with two independent calculations: a
primary in-process analytic implementation and a fully BFS-free
cross-check. This report documents the analytic implementation, which is
the BFS \emph{unstable-W} EFT expansion of Beneke, Falgari, Schwinn and
collaborators, established in their NLO paper~\cite{BFSnlo} and the
dominant-NNLO follow-up~\cite{BFSnnlo}; the independent cross-check ---
a MoCaNLO\,$+$\,Recola NLO-EW four-fermion line shape that shares no code
or perturbative input with the EFT chain --- is presented in
section~\ref{sec:indep}, and an exploratory matching of the BFS
higher-order corrections onto it in section~\ref{sec:match}.

The report is organised in two parts. \textbf{Part~\ref{part:impl}}
documents the calculation and its closure: the equations actually
evaluated by the code (section~\ref{sec:impl}) and the round-trips that
confirm the implementation reproduces every table of the two BFS papers
we currently rely on (section~\ref{sec:val}). \textbf{Part~\ref{part:results}}
reports the physics: the FCC-ee sensitivity and the statistical,
experimental- and theory-systematic budget on $(\mW,\GammaW)$ --- the
incremental theory-piece ladder, the ISR-scheme uncertainty, the
scan-scenario comparison, the channel extrapolation, and the independent
MoCaNLO cross-check --- closing with the limitations and open items
(section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{The BFS unstable-particle EFT in one page}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

BFS organise the threshold cross section as a double expansion in the
electroweak coupling $\alpha$ and the resonance scaling
$\delta\sim\GammaW/\mW \sim v^2$, where $v$ is the non-relativistic
$W$-velocity. Counting $\delta\sim\alpha$, the partonic four-fermion
cross section reads
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma(s) \;=\; \sigma^{(0)} \;+\; \sigma^{(1/2)} \;+\; \sigma^{(1)}
   \;+\; \sigma^{(3/2)} \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\delta^2),
\label{eq:bfs-expansion}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{(p/2)}$ collects everything of relative order $\delta^{p/2}$
($p\!=\!0,1,2,3$ are usually called $\mathrm{LO}$, $\mathrm{N^{1/2}LO}$,
$\mathrm{NLO}$, $\mathrm{N^{3/2}LO}$ in the EFT counting). Going beyond
$N^{3/2}\text{LO}_\text{EFT}$ requires the dominant pieces of
$\sigma^{(2)}$ identified in Ref.~\cite{BFSnnlo} (``NNLO'' in this
report to match the BFS-NNLO paper convention).

The natural BFS quantity is the LR-helicity cross section for the
\emph{specific} channel $e^+e^-\to\munuqq$,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm LR}^{(p/2)}(s;\mW,\GammaW),
\end{equation}
in which the leptonic and hadronic $W$ branching fractions are encoded
through an explicit prefactor proportional to $1/27$ (BFS line 1130).
For the analysis we need the unpolarised initial state and the inclusive
$\mu\nu q\bar q$ final state, both of which are derived from
$\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(p/2)}$ by trivial helicity and flavour multipliers
(section~\ref{sec:born}).

When the propagator width $\GammaW$ is the NLO+QCD value rather than the
LO theoretical width $\GammaWzero$, the explicit $1/27$ in the BFS Born
formulae must be replaced by $(\GammaWzero/\GammaW)^2 / 27$ for the doubly
resonant piece and by $(\GammaWzero/\GammaW)/27$ for the singly resonant
piece (BFS section~6.1 / eq.~(83)). The code applies this BR correction
component-by-component, see section~\ref{sec:born}.

\subsection{Code organisation}\label{sec:codeorg}

The BFS chain lives under \code{framework/process/ww/xsec_calculator/}:
\begin{description}
\item[\code{eft_xsec.py}] EW couplings ($s_W^2$ OS, $\alpha_{G_\mu}(\mW)$),
   the BFS-paper reference constants, the partonic specific-channel
   driver $\code{sigma\_partonic\_munuqq}$, and the (off-by-default)
   multiplicative FKM $K_C$ Coulomb factor.
\item[\code{bfs_eft.py}] All closed-form BFS contributions: Born
   expansion (eqs.~17, 33, 37, 38, 39, 40 of \cite{BFSnlo}), NLO
   loop corrections (HSC bracket, NLO Coulomb eq.~62, EW decay), and
   the dominant NNLO pieces (eq.~49 of \cite{BFSnnlo}). Also the
   Whizard-anchor factor and the $\delta_\text{QCD}$ multiplier.
\item[\code{bfs_c1fin.py}] The analytic NLO hard-matching coefficients
   $c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}$, $c_{d,l}$, $c_{d,h}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}$ as
   closed-form functions of $(\mW,m_t,M_H,M_Z)$ (cf.~Scenario~H).
\item[\code{isr.py}] ISR convolution: the production eMELA NLL radiator
   (DELTA factorisation) and the LL$+$exp BETA-scheme radiator
   (single-convolution shortcut and BFS-style two-leg double
   convolution), the latter kept for the BFS-table closure reruns.
\item[\code{emela_wrapper.py}] Thin wrapper around the eMELA NLL
   electron ePDF $D(x,Q)$ consumed by the two-leg ISR convolution.
\item[\code{grid_morph.py}, \code{whizard_grid.py}] The Whizard
   exact-Born anchor: the per-$\sqrts$ quadratic morphing predictor
   (trained on the fine Whizard grid) and the raw grid loader.
\end{description}

The public assembly functions are:
\begin{description}
\item[$\code{sigma\_BFS\_specific\_munuud\_pb}(s,\mW,\GammaW;\ldots)$]
   sums all enabled BFS pieces for the specific channel $\munuqq$.
\item[$\code{sigma\_observed\_munuqq}(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW;\ldots)$]
   ISR-convoluted observed cross section, $\mu\nu q\bar q$ or per
   channel, exposing the chain via keyword toggles.
\end{description}
The defaults of \code{sigma_observed_munuqq} are the project's ``best
calculation'': full BFS Born $+$ NLO loops $+$ NNLO $+$ $\delta_\text{QCD}$
$+$ Whizard anchor $+$ single-convolution eMELA-NLL ISR
(DELTA factorisation, ALPMZ renormalisation, $\alpha(M_Z)\!=\!1/128.943$;
cf.~section~\ref{sec:isr}). The card knob \code{isr_nll=True} became
production default on 2026-05-29 after the cross-fit campaign of
section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross} quantified the LL$+$exp residual bias
at $\pm 22.4\MeV$, $\sim 90\times$ the FCC-ee target
$\sigma(\mW)\!=\!0.25\MeV$. The legacy LL$+$exp BETA radiator
(\code{isr_nll=False}) is kept available for paper-closure tests
that match BFS's own structure-function choice
(cf.~section~\ref{sec:val-table4}).

\paragraph{Theory inputs (production vs.\ paper closure).} The EW
parameters $\alpha_s(\mW)$, $m_t$, $M_H$, and $M_Z$ are card-driven
through \code{cards/ww\_default.py:PARAM\_INPUTS} and threaded
end-to-end into every Born, NLO, NNLO, and anchor piece (since
2026-05-27). Production defaults track PDG / FCC-ee FSR
($\alpha_s(\mW)=0.1199$, $m_t=172.5\GeV$ from FSR
Table~2~\cite{FCCFSR2025}, $M_H=125.25\GeV$, $M_Z=91.1876\GeV$).
The closure scripts of
section~\ref{sec:val} pin to the BFS-paper reference set
($m_t=174.2\GeV$, $M_H=115\GeV$ pre-discovery, $M_Z=91.188\GeV$ to
3~sig.\ figs.) via the framework constants \code{M\_T\_BFS\_REF},
\code{M\_H\_BFS\_REF}, \code{M\_Z\_BFS\_REF} exposed by
\code{eft\_xsec.py}, so paper closure is preserved as production
defaults track PDG. The dominant slope is
$\partial\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}/\partial M_H\approx
-0.022/\GeV$ (Scenario~H of section~\ref{sec:val-loops}), so the
$115\to 125.25\GeV$ shift moves $\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}$ by
$\sim 0.23$, a few-permille effect on $\sigma_\text{NLO}$. These slopes are also
why $m_t$ and $M_H$ carry no relevant \emph{parametric} uncertainty here: the
threshold $\mW$ extraction is a direct kinematic measurement of the WW production
turn-on, immune to the SM $\Delta r(m_t,M_H)$ loop relation that drives the
indirect $\mW$-from-EW-fit determination, so $m_t$/$M_H$ enter only through these
sub-permille $\sigma$-chain slopes. With the FCC-ee projected input precisions
($\delta m_t\!\sim\!6.5\MeV$, $\delta M_H\!\sim\!4\MeV$) the induced $\mW$ shift is
$\ll0.01\MeV$; \code{PARAM\_UNC} for $m_t$, $M_H$, $M_Z$ and the $\sigma$-chain
$\alpha_{\rm em}$ is therefore set to \code{None} (documented, not profiled), and
only $\alpha_s$ and $\alpha_{\rm em,ISR}$ are carried as parametric nuisances.

\paragraph{The $\alpha_{\rm em}$ inputs and EW scheme.}\label{par:alpha-em}
Since 2026-05-27 \code{PARAM\_INPUTS} also exposes two
$\alpha_{\rm em}$ overrides, both defaulting to \code{None}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{alpha\_em} — the $\alpha$ used in the $\sigma$ chain
   (Born + NLO + NNLO loops).\quad\code{None} $\to$ derived
   $\alpha_{G_\mu}(\mW, M_Z) = \sqrt{2}\,G_F\,\mW^2\,\sin^2\theta_W^{\rm OS}/\pi$
   at the fit-point $\mW$.
\item \code{alpha\_em\_isr} — the $\alpha$ used to build the LL+exp
   ISR exponent $\beta_e = (2\alpha/\pi)(L_e-1)$ (and propagated as
   the eMELA \code{ALPMZ} renormalisation input when NLL is engaged).
   Production card default since 2026-05-28 is $\alpha(M_Z)=1/128.943$
   (PDG; FCC-ee target via $A_\text{FB}^{\mu\mu}$
   off-peak~\cite{RiembauTeraZ}) --- the value the soft/collinear
   photon physically sees and the natural input for eMELA's DGLAP
   evolution.\quad\code{None} $\to$ legacy fallback
   $\alpha_{G_\mu}(M_W^{\rm BFS\,ref})\approx 1/132.1$, frozen across the
   fit (BFS prescription, line 2514 of \cite{BFSnlo}); kept for
   paper-closure tests.
\end{itemize}
Implemented via an \code{override=} kwarg on \code{alpha\_Gmu(...)} and
threaded as \code{alpha\_em} through every $\sigma$ helper that
contains an explicit $\alpha$ factor; the convolution-internal
\code{alpha\_em} kwarg in \code{sigma\_ISR\_*\_convolution} was renamed
\code{alpha\_em\_isr} to free up \code{alpha\_em} as the
$\sigma$-chain key forwarded through \code{**sigma\_kwargs}. With both
\code{None} the calculation is byte-identical to the previous chain.
The two couplings enter $\mW$ differently. $\alpha_{\rm em,ISR}$ enters the
ISR radiator exponent $\beta_e$ as a genuine \emph{shape} effect, capable of
biasing $\mW$; its input uncertainty $\Delta\alpha(M_Z)=4.7\times 10^{-8}$
(FCC-ee combined Tera-Z projection,~\cite{RiembauTeraZ}) is profiled as the
Gaussian-constraint nuisance \code{aem\_isr}, $\mW$ impact $\approx0.005\MeV$
(the $\alpha_{\rm em,ISR}$ row of Table~\ref{tab:scenario-syst}, sweep in
Fig.~\ref{fig:scenario-scan-mW}). The $\sigma$-chain coupling
$\alpha_{G_\mu}(\mW,M_Z)$ instead enters through $G_F$ as an overall
\emph{normalisation} ($\sigma\propto\alpha^2$): a shift does \emph{not} bias
$\mW$ --- it is degenerate with luminosity, orthogonal to $\mW$ in the mean ---
so \code{PARAM\_UNC.alpha\_em} stays \code{None}. But because $\mW$ is extracted
substantially from the \emph{rate} (the shape-only fit inflates $\sigma(\mW)$
from $1.36$ to $4.71\MeV$ once the rate is removed), this normalisation
uncertainty still \emph{inflates} $\sigma(\mW)$ --- it is not orthogonal to
$\mW$ in the covariance. It is therefore profiled as a separate \emph{flat
normalisation} nuisance \code{aemEW}, with prior $\delta\sigma/\sigma =
1.2\times 10^{-5} = 2\,\delta\alpha/\alpha$ from the same Riembau $\alpha(M_Z)$
(modelled as a flat rescale rather than an $\alpha_{\rm em}$ override, since the
$\alpha$-blind WHIZARD anchor cancels the leading $\alpha^2$ Born dependence).
Its Asimov $\mW$ impact is $\approx0.03\MeV$ with $\rho(\mW,\code{aemEW})\approx
+0.02$ (the $\alpha_{\rm em,EW}$ row of Table~\ref{tab:scenario-syst}). Both
$\alpha$ \emph{input} nuisances are far below the $0.25\MeV$ target and are
distinct from the NLL ISR \emph{renormalisation-scheme} variation
(ALPMZ$\leftrightarrow$ALGMU$\leftrightarrow\alpha(0)$), a separate theory
uncertainty measured in section~\ref{sec:val-isr-scheme}.

The whole chain is in the $G_\mu$ + OS scheme: $\alpha_{G_\mu}$ is
defined to absorb the leading $\Delta r$ correction, and
$\sin^2\theta_W = 1-(\mW/M_Z)^2$ ties the weak mixing angle to the
pole mass. A switch to an $\alpha(M_Z)$ + $\sin^2\theta_W^{\overline{\rm MS}}$
four-input scheme (independent EW inputs aligned with FCC-ee
Tera-Z measurements~\cite{RiembauTeraZ}) has been investigated and
parked; revisit conditions and the literature map for a future
implementation are recorded internally.

\subsection{Born expansion}\label{sec:born}

The Born sum implemented in \code{bfs_eft.py} for the specific channel
$\munuqq$ is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^\text{Born} \;=\;
\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(0)} \,+\, \sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(1/2)} \,+\,
\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(1)} \,+\, \sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(3/2),a},
\end{equation}
plus the analogous $RL$ sum (no $RL^{(0)}$ contribution: see
Ref.~\cite{BFSnlo} section 4.1). The four pieces correspond to:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(0)}$ — doubly resonant LO, eq.~(17) of
   \cite{BFSnlo}, evaluated with the complex-velocity
   $\beta(s)=\sqrt{(s-4\mW^2+i\Gamma_W\,\cdot 4\mW)/s}$. Coded in
   \code{sigma_LR0_specific_pb}.
\item $\sigma_\mathrm{LR,RL}^{(1)}$ — NLO subleading potential
   (single-Coulomb-exchange residue), eq.~(33). The
   $\GammaW^\text{NLO}$ EW shift inside eq.~(33) (the
   $\GammaW \to \GammaW(1+\delta_\text{decay}^\text{(1,ew)})$
   replacement) is dropped here: $\GammaW$ is a free fit parameter, not
   split into $\GammaW^{(0)}+\GammaW^{(1)}$, so the decay-side EW
   correction enters the chain once only, via the NLO-decay term
   \code{delta_sigma_NLO_decay_specific_pb} (no double counting).
\item $\sigma_\mathrm{LR,RL}^{(1/2)}$ — hard non-resonant, eqs.~(37)+(38)
   plus the appendix-A $C^f_{h_i,h}\,K^f_{h_i}$ coefficients for $h_4$–$h_7$.
   Coded in \code{sigma_LR_RL_half_specific_pb}; the $K$-coefficients
   $K^f_{h_4}=\{-0.266477, +0.190394\}$ etc.\ are pasted from BFS lines
   3116–3148.
\item $\sigma_\mathrm{LR,RL}^{(3/2),a}$ — energy-dependent $N^{3/2}$LO,
   eqs.~(39)+(40), in \code{sigma_LR_RL_three_half_a_specific_pb}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pieces deliberately omitted.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sigma^{(3/2),b}$ (eq.~41) — the paper estimates its size at
   $\lesssim 2\fb$, energy-independent. Empirically our Born sum
   reproduces the BFS Table~1 $N^{3/2}\mathrm{LO}_\mathrm{EFT}$ column
   to 4–5 digits at every $\sqrts$ in the scan window
   (section~\ref{sec:val-born}), which bounds the size of any missing
   piece well below $0.01\fb$.
\item $\sigma_\mathrm{RL}^{(1)}$ at NLO loop level — drops at NLO
   (Ref.~\cite{BFSnlo} section 4.1: no LR/RL helicity interference at
   one loop).
\item $\mathrm{Im}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}$ — paper section 4.2
   above eq.~(53): for the flavour-specific cross section only
   $\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}$ contributes.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{BR-correction convention.} The BFS Born formulae carry a
$1/27$ flavour factor that assumes the LO theoretical $W$ width
$\GammaWzero(\mW)=3\alpha\,\mW/(4 s_W^2)$ in the propagator (eq.~19).
When the propagator uses an NLO+QCD width $\GammaW\neq\GammaWzero$, BFS
eq.~(83) instructs the user to multiply each piece by an appropriate
power of $\GammaWzero/\GammaW$: \emph{two} cut propagators (doubly
resonant) get $(\GammaWzero/\GammaW)^2$, \emph{one} cut propagator
(singly resonant) gets $(\GammaWzero/\GammaW)$. Our
\code{_BR_correction(\mW,\GammaW)} encodes the squared factor; the
linear vs.\ squared assignment is done by the assembly function
$\code{sigma\_BFS\_specific\_munuud\_pb}$ piece by piece. Without this
correction the BFS Table~2 prediction is $\sim$4–5\% too large.

\subsection{NLO loop corrections}\label{sec:nlo}

The NLO content of Ref.~\cite{BFSnlo} section 4 is split into three
additive pieces, all applied to $\sigma_\mathrm{LR}$ only:

\paragraph{Hard $+$ soft $+$ collinear (HSC).}
The bracket term of eq.~(79) of~\cite{BFSnlo},
\begin{equation}
\Delta\hat\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(1,\mathrm{HSC})} =
\frac{4\alpha^3}{27\,s_W^4 s}\,
\mathrm{Im}\!\left\{-\sqrt{z}\Big[2\ln(4z)+
\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})} +
\tfrac{\pi^2}{4} + \tfrac{1}{2}\Big]\right\},
\quad z=-(E+i\GammaW)/\mW,\;E=\sqrts-2\mW,
\end{equation}
is the residue of the hard $+$ soft $+$ collinear corrections after all
$1/\epsilon^2$ and $1/\epsilon$ poles cancel against each other and
against the LL-ePDF scheme conversion. Coded in
\code{delta_sigma_NLO_hard_softcoll_specific_pb}. The hard matching
coefficient, evaluated analytically as a closed-form function of
$(\mW,m_t,M_H,M_Z)$ in \code{bfs_c1fin.py}, equals
$\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}=-10.076$ at the BFS
reference inputs (BFS line 1797); its $\mW$ dependence is sub-percent on
$\sigma^{(1,\mathrm{HSC})}$ in the fit range, see
section~\ref{sec:val-loops} and the Scenario~H closure.

\paragraph{NLO Coulomb potential.}
Eq.~(62) of \cite{BFSnlo}, the $\alpha\,\alpha_\text{em}^2/s$
single-Coulomb-correction term plus the two-photon subleading piece.
Coded in \code{delta_sigma_Coulomb_NLO_specific_pb}.

\paragraph{NLO electroweak decay.}
Ref.~\cite{BFSnlo} eq.~(84),
\begin{equation}
\Delta\sigma_\mathrm{decay}^{(1,\mathrm{ew})} =
\Big(\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{(1,\mathrm{ew})}/\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{(0)} +
     \Gamma_{ud}^{(1,\mathrm{ew})}/\Gamma_{ud}^{(0)}\Big)\,
\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(0)},
\label{eq:nlo-decay}
\end{equation}
with the $\Gamma^{(1,\mathrm{ew})}_{W}$ bracket of eq.~(60) of \cite{BFSnlo} evaluated with
$c_{d,\ell}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}=-2.709$, $c_{d,h}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}=-2.034$
at the BFS reference inputs (BFS line 1920; both are analytic functions
of $(\mW,m_t,M_H,M_Z)$ in \code{bfs_c1fin.py}). At the BFS reference
parameters the bracket equals
$-0.71\%$ (purely EW; QCD content is captured by $\delta_\text{QCD}$
below — including both would double-count). Coded in
\code{delta_sigma_NLO_decay_specific_pb}.

\subsection{Dominant NNLO}\label{sec:nnlo}

The dominant $N^{3/2}\text{LO}_\text{EFT}$ partonic correction of
Ref.~\cite{BFSnnlo} eq.~(49) is the sum
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^{(3/2)} =
\Delta\hat\sigma^{(C\times[S+H])}_\mathrm{LR} +
\Delta\sigma^{(\text{NLO-}C)}_\mathrm{LR} +
\Delta\sigma^{(C\times\text{decay})}_\mathrm{LR} +
\Delta\sigma^{(C\times\text{res})}_\mathrm{LR} +
\Delta\sigma^{(C3)}_\mathrm{LR}.
\end{equation}
All five admit closed forms in the paper and are coded in
\code{bfs_eft.py}:
\begin{description}
\item[$\Delta\hat\sigma^{(C\times[S+H])}$ (eq.~34)] interference of
   single-Coulomb exchange with the soft+hard NLO corrections,
   $\propto \alpha_\mathrm{ew}^2 \alpha^2/(27 s)$, with bracket
   $9 + \pi^2/2 + 2\,\mathrm{Re}\,c_{p,\mathrm{LR}}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}$
   times $\mathrm{Im}\,\ln(-\mathcal{E}_W/\mW)$ plus
   $2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\ln^2(-\mathcal{E}_W/\mW)$.
\item[$\Delta\sigma^{(\text{NLO-}C)}$ (eq.~39)]
   $\alpha(M_Z)$-scheme piece (photon-bubble running of $\alpha$ on the
   Coulomb photon) plus the $\alpha(M_Z)\to G_\mu$ conversion
   $\delta_{\alpha(M_Z)\to G_\mu}=4.103\,\alpha$. Uses the photon
   vacuum-polarisation sum $\sum_f C_f Q_f^2 = 20/3$ (top integrated out).
\item[$\Delta\sigma^{(C\times\text{decay})}$ (eq.~40)] same EW-decay
   bracket as $\Delta\sigma^{(1,\mathrm{ew})}_\text{decay}$ times the
   single-Coulomb piece $\Delta\sigma^{(C1)}$.
\item[$\Delta\sigma^{(C\times\text{res})}$ (eq.~48)] interference of
   residue correction and single-Coulomb exchange, $\propto
   \alpha_\mathrm{ew}^2 \alpha / (27 s) \cdot \GammaW/\mW$ times an
   $|\mathcal{E}_W|$-dependent logarithm; dominant NNLO piece above
   threshold ($\sim 1.5\fb$ at $170\GeV$).
\item[$\Delta\sigma^{(C3)}$ (eq.~11)] triple-Coulomb exchange,
   $\propto \alpha^3\,\zeta(3)\,\mathrm{Im}[-\mW/\mathcal{E}_W]$;
   tiny ($\lesssim 0.01\fb$ helicity-averaged).
\end{description}
The total NNLO impact on $\mW$ is $\sim$3\MeV{} after ISR (BFS sec.~6),
$\sim$5\MeV{} partonic. The $\hat\cdot$ on $\Delta\hat\sigma^{(C\times[S+H])}$
denotes that the IR poles have been subtracted by the LL ePDF in
$\overline{\rm MS}$ — the BFS paper instructs that this partonic piece
must be further ISR-convoluted (section~\ref{sec:val-nnlo-isr}).

\subsection{$\delta_\text{QCD}$ multiplier}\label{sec:dqcd}

The universal QCD correction to hadronic partial widths,
\begin{equation}
\delta_\text{QCD}(\alpha_s) = 1 + \frac{\alpha_s}{\pi} +
1.409\,\Big(\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\Big)^2,
\end{equation}
multiplies the full NLO electroweak cross section. BFS section 6.1
(lines 2622–2637) shows that doing so reproduces the QCD running of
hadronic partial widths to NNLO precision. At $\alpha_s(\mW)=0.1199$
this is $+4.0\%$. Coded in \code{delta_QCD_factor}. The QCD content of
$\delta_\text{decay}^{(1)}$ is intentionally not included in the EW
\code{delta_decay_EW_relative} to avoid double counting.

\subsection{Whizard exact-Born anchor}\label{sec:anchor}

BFS section 6.2 prescribes a final ``anchor'' to the Whizard exact 4-fermion
Born: the BFS Born EFT truncation, summed and BR-corrected, is multiplied
by
\begin{equation}
f(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW) \;=\;
\frac{\sigma_\mathrm{Whizard}^\text{exact Born}(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW)}
     {\sigma_\mathrm{LR}^\text{Born,BFS}(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW)},
\end{equation}
which captures the difference between the EFT $N^{3/2}\mathrm{LO}$
truncation and the exact 4-fermion Born within the same propagator
prescription. The anchor is applied to the Born sum \emph{only}, not to
the NLO loops or NNLO additions, which are added on top, following the
BFS NLO master cross-section formula (eq.~(79) of~\cite{BFSnlo}).

Three interchangeable sources for $\sigma_\mathrm{Whizard}^\text{exact Born}$
are wired in and selectable via \code{NLO\_CONFIG["whizard\_anchor\_source"]}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{morph} (production default) — the per-$\sqrts$ analytic
  morph of section~\ref{sec:morphing}, fitted from a dedicated
  6363-point \texttt{grid\_fine} WHIZARD~3.1.5 campaign
  ($9\,\mW \times 7\,\GammaW \times 101\,\sqrts$ at 0.1-GeV $\sqrts$
  step in $[155, 165]$~GeV, $\sim$0.008\,\% MC stat per point, i.e.\
  25-MeV $\mW$ step), plus the 0.5-GeV outer wings from the highstats
  campaign as $\sqrts$-spline support beyond the fine window. A held-out
  sub-MeV $(\mW,\GammaW)$ campaign at $\sim$0.005\,\% MC bounds the
  morph's interpolation bias at the fit's working resolution
  (Appendix~\ref{sec:morphval}).
\item \texttt{grid} — trilinear interpolation in $(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW)$
  on a 1295-point WHIZARD~3.1.5 scan
  ($5\,\mW \times 7\,\GammaW \times 37\,\sqrts$, $\sim$0.05\,\% MC stat
  per point) produced by the pipeline in \texttt{WW\_threshold/whizard/};
  diagnostic alternative, the predecessor of the production
  \texttt{morph}.
\item \texttt{spline} — cubic in $\delta = \sqrts - 2\mW$ and
  linear in $\GammaW$ between the two BFS reference points
  ($\GammaW = 2.04483$ and $2.09201$~GeV) from Tables~1 and~2 of
  arXiv:0707.0773; retained as the BFS-paper-closure fallback used
  by the historical Scenario~I validation.
\end{itemize}
WHIZARD computes the specific channel $\mu^-\bar\nu_\mu u\bar d$ with
SM-LO partial widths fixed by the EW input scheme, so as the propagator
$\GammaW$ is scanned the implicit
$(\Gamma_\text{partial}/\GammaW)^2$ branching ratio shrinks. For the
PDG-constant BR convention used by the fit, that squeeze is undone by a
fixed BR-strip factor
$(\GammaW / \Gamma_W^{(0)}(M_W^\text{BFS,ref}))^2$ in the anchor's
numerator — fixed reference (not the running $\Gamma_W^{(0)}(\mW)$) so
the strip is $\mW$-independent and the BR sector knows nothing about the
fit $\mW$. The chain then multiplies by the PDG-measured
$\mathrm{BR}_\text{inclusive}$ to obtain $\sigma_\text{observed}$. PDG
branching ratios are primitive constants in the steering card; their
products \texttt{BR\_INCLUSIVE\_MUNUQQ} and \texttt{BR\_MUNUUD} are
derived once in \code{eft_xsec.py}.

Closure of $\sigma_\mathrm{BFS}\!\cdot\!f$ against the BFS Whizard
reference column is shown in section~\ref{sec:val-anchor}.

\subsubsection{Morphing scheme (fine grid)}\label{sec:morphing}

For the dedicated \texttt{grid\_fine} campaign (0.1-GeV $\sqrts$ step
across the analysis window at $\sim$0.008\,\% MC) the operational
predictor is not a trilinear interpolation of $\sigma_\mathrm{Whizard}$
directly but a per-$\sqrts$ analytic morph fitted from the grid:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\text{pred}(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW) =
\sigma_0(\sqrts)\,
\mathcal{R}_m\!\left(\sqrts,\mW\right)\,
\mathcal{R}_\Gamma\!\left(\sqrts,\GammaW\right)\,
\bigl[1 + \beta(\sqrts)\,(\mW-\mW^0)(\GammaW-\GammaW^0)\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $\mW^0=80.379$\,GeV, $\GammaW^0=2.085$\,GeV. At each grid $\sqrts$:
$\mathcal{R}_m$ and $\mathcal{R}_\Gamma$ are quadratic LSQ fits along the
respective 1D axis at nominal value of the other parameter; $\beta(\sqrts)$
is a single scalar fitted from the doubly-off-axis residual to absorb the
joint $(\mW,\GammaW)$ coupling. Each of the eight $\sqrts$-dependent
quantities ($\sigma_0$, three coefficients each of $\mathcal{R}_m$ and
$\mathcal{R}_\Gamma$, $\beta$) is then carried along the dense $\sqrts$
axis with a natural cubic spline --- after the grid has been denoised
in $\sqrts$ so the spline threads clean points rather than propagating
Monte-Carlo fluctuations (Appendix~\ref{sec:morphval}).

The bilinear cross-term coefficient peaks at $\beta\simeq+0.29$~GeV$^{-2}$
at $\sqrts\approx160.5$~GeV (the threshold rise, where the $\mW$
threshold-position shift and the $\GammaW$-driven smearing are most
coupled) and decays to $\sim\!0$ above the peak. Naive independent
quadratic morphing (without $\beta$) leaves a $\sim$0.13\,\% residual at
the worst $\sqrts$; the cross term reduces it to the MC-bar
($\sim$0.03\,\%) over the analysis window. Leave-one-out
cross-validation on the $\sqrts$ axis confirms the cubic spline along
$\sqrts$ adds no precision overhead: the median LOO residual at
interior $\sqrts$ sits at the per-point MC bar (0.005\,\%), the maximum
below $0.1\,\%$ at the peak-rise. Two held-out WHIZARD campaigns
bound the closure: a 1-MeV ($\mW,\GammaW$) plane at 3 $\sqrts$
(\texttt{grid\_validate}) and 1D ($\mW$, $\GammaW$) scans at sub-MeV
spacing on three $\sqrts$ slices (\texttt{grid\_validate\_fine}), the
latter at $\sim$0.005\,\% MC. The morph reproduces both at the MC bar
(maximum residual 0.04\,\% and 0.02\,\% respectively), so the morph
interpolation is sub-MeV-safe in the regime the fit operates in. The
construction is taken apart and validated extensively in
Appendix~\ref{sec:morphval}: the input grid, the per-$\sqrts$
quadratic fits, the bilinear cross term, the $\sqrts$ interpolation,
held-out and external (BFS-table) closure, and physics-sanity checks.
Diagnostics under
\code{scripts/investigations/whizard_grid_highstats/}.

\subsection{ISR convolution}\label{sec:isr}

We use the LL$+$exp electron structure function in the BETA scheme of
Skrzypek~\cite{Skrzypek1992} and
Cacciari-Deandrea-Montagna-Nicrosini~\cite{CDMN1992}, with full
$\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ exponentiation of the soft$+$virtual endpoint
(LEP2 Yellow Report, Beenakker et al.~\cite{LEP2YRww}). Two equivalent
forms are implemented:

\paragraph{Single-convolution shortcut (default).}
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\text{obs}(s) = \int_{z_\text{min}}^1 \!\mathrm{d}z\,
H(z;s)\,\hat\sigma(z\,s),
\qquad
H(z;s) = H_\text{SV}(\beta)\,\beta(1-z)^{\beta-1} + H_\text{NS}(z;\beta),
\end{equation}
with $\beta=(2\alpha/\pi)(\ln(s/m_e^2)-1)$. This is the LEP2 YR
$\alpha\to 2\alpha$ shortcut that collapses the two-leg form into one
variable $z=x_1 x_2$. Coded in \code{sigma_ISR_convolution}.

\paragraph{Two-leg form, BFS prescription.}
The BFS-paper convolution,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\text{obs}(s)=\int_0^1\!\mathrm{d}x_1\!\int_0^1\!\mathrm{d}x_2\,
\Gamma_{ee}(x_1)\,\Gamma_{ee}(x_2)\,\hat\sigma(x_1 x_2 s),
\end{equation}
with per-leg BETA structure function $\Gamma_{ee}(x;\beta)$ (LEP2 YR
explicit form). Matches BFS eq.~(71) and is the form used by BFS for
their Tables 4–5. Coded in \code{sigma_ISR_2leg_convolution}.

The two forms are formally equivalent at LL$+$exp; they differ at NLL
by a few permille around $\sqrts\!=\!161\GeV$. BFS (page~41 around
eq.~88) cite this as a residual ISR systematic of
$\delta\mW\!\approx\!31\MeV$, the motivation for the NLL upgrade
discussed in section~\ref{sec:limits}. Per-leg validation against BFS
Table~4 (the 2-leg reference) is below 0.5\% at 158–170 GeV; the
single-convolution form (the default value of the \code{isr\_scheme}
field, though the production chain with \code{isr\_nll=True} auto-promotes
to the 2-leg eMELA NLL path) agrees with the 2-leg LL$+$exp form to
better than 0.1\% on the scan window after the integration substitution
$u=(1-z)^\beta$ that smooths the soft endpoint and a
$n_\text{quad}=200$ Gauss-Legendre rule --- an LL$+$exp internal
cross-check, not the production ISR.

\paragraph{The BFS prescription and where it differs from ours.}
A careful read of \cite{BFSnlo} lines 2645--2666 (the paragraph just
before BFS Table~3) reveals that the BFS authors did \emph{not} perform
eq.~(71) — the explicit LL$+$exp 2-leg convolution — for their
Born$\otimes$ISR column. Instead:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Born(ISR) column} (BFS Table~3 col.~2 / Table~4 col.~2):
  raw WHIZARD output with WHIZARD's own ISR structure function (Skrzypek
  1990, multiplicative form — see below). No partonic
  $\sqrt{x_1x_2 s}$ cut.
\item \textbf{NLO addition} (BFS Table~3 col.~3 / Table~4 col.~3):
  WHIZARD Born(ISR) \emph{plus} an explicit LL$+$exp 2-leg convolution
  of the analytic $\Delta\hat\sigma_{\rm NLO}$, with a partonic
  $\sqrt{x_1 x_2 s} > 155\,\text{GeV}$ cut. BFS state that the
  cut-value dependence is negligible (variations of $\pm 5\,\text{GeV}$
  shift the integral by $\lesssim 0.1\,\%$, BFS lines 2664--2666).
\item Inside the analytic $\Delta\hat\sigma_{\rm NLO}$, BFS replace
  $\hat\sigma_{LR}^{(0)}$ by the full Born $\hat\sigma_{\rm Born}$ in
  the decay correction $\Delta\hat\sigma_{\rm decay}^{(1,\rm ew)}$
  (eq.~49 of \cite{BFSnlo}; line 2660 of that paper's source). This is
  a non-trivial recipe choice — it injects the BFS-EFT$\to$full-4f
  matching into the decay piece — and its size is comparable to the
  rest of the recipe-level residual (see
  section~\ref{sec:val-table4-budget}).
\end{itemize}
Our framework follows a \emph{different but consistent} recipe: a
single LL$+$exp 2-leg convolution applied to (anchored Born sum) $+$
(analytic NLO), with the Whizard anchor at the \emph{partonic} level
(section~\ref{sec:anchor}). The two recipes converge below 0.5\,\% on
the scan window (BFS Table~4); the decomposition of the residual is
the subject of section~\ref{sec:val-table4-budget}.

\paragraph{WHIZARD's ISR is not exactly Beenakker BETA LL$+$exp.}
WHIZARD~3.1.5 builds the electron PDF \emph{multiplicatively}
(\code{src/qed\_pdf/electron\_pdfs\_sub.f90} routine
\code{qed\_pdf\_evolve\_qed\_pdf}, lines 89--127):
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{ee}^{\rm WHIZARD}(x;\epsilon) =
   \underbrace{\bigl[\,1 + g_1\,\epsilon + g_2\,\epsilon^2 + g_3\,\epsilon^3\,\bigr]}_{\text{multiplicative SV part}}
   \cdot \prod_{k\ge 1}\!
   \underbrace{\bigl[\,1 - \tfrac{1}{8\,(1-x)}\,B_k(x)\,\epsilon^k\,\bigr]}_{\text{multiplicative NS-hard correction}}
   \cdot \Gamma^{(0)}(x;\epsilon),
\label{eq:whizard-isr}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon = (\alpha/\pi)\,(L_e-1)$, $L_e = \ln(s/m_e^2)$
($\epsilon=\beta/2$ in the Beenakker convention of
eq.~\eqref{eq:beenakker-beta}), $g_1 = 3/4$,
$g_2 = (27-8\pi^2)/96$, $g_3 = (27-24\pi^2+128\,\zeta_3)/384$, and
$B_k(x)$ the polynomial brackets in the standard
LEP2~YR~\cite{LEP2YRww} eq.~(67) BETA scheme. Beenakker eq.~(67) is
\emph{additive}:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{ee}^{\rm BETA}(x;\beta) =
\tfrac{e^{\beta(3/8-\gamma_E/2)}}{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)}\,\tfrac{\beta}{2}\,(1-x)^{\beta/2-1}
\;-\;\tfrac{\beta}{4}(1{+}x)
\;-\;\tfrac{\beta^2}{32}\,B_2(x)
\;-\;\tfrac{\beta^3}{384}\,B_3(x).
\label{eq:beenakker-beta}
\end{equation}
The two coincide at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^1)$ but diverge at
$\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$: WHIZARD effectively resums a subset of
NLL-type hard$\times$soft cross-terms that LL$+$exp does not. On the
$\sigma_{\rm obs}$ side this is a $\sim 0.3$--$1.3\,\%$ recipe-level
difference at $\sqrts\in[161,170]\GeV$, growing toward $\sim 2\,\%$ at
$158\GeV$ where ISR explores below-threshold partonic energies most
aggressively. Quantitative budget in
section~\ref{sec:val-table4-budget}.

\paragraph{$\alpha$ choice in the ISR exponent.}
We follow BFS line 2514: $\alpha=\alpha_{G_\mu}$ everywhere, evaluated
at the BFS reference $\mW=80.377\GeV$ \emph{not} at the fit $\mW$. The
ISR scale is set by the soft/collinear photon, not by the $W$ resonance;
running $\alpha$ with $\mW$ would inject a fictitious $\mW$ dependence
in the radiator. The default is hard-wired in \code{isr.py};
\code{beta_ISR} accepts an explicit $\alpha_\text{em}$ override for
diagnostics.

\paragraph{NLL ISR via eMELA (2026-05).}
The principled NLL upgrade is now available through the
eMELA~library~\cite{eMELA2019}, which solves DGLAP in Mellin space at
NLL accuracy and returns the electron ePDF $D(x,Q)$ in the DELTA
factorisation scheme. The 2-leg convolution
\code{sigma\_ISR\_2leg\_convolution} accepts:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{nll=True} --- the full NLL ePDF in the production default:
  DELTA factorisation, \emph{ALPMZ} renormalisation,
  $\alpha=\alpha(M_Z)=1/128.943$ (the value FCC-ee will measure to
  $\Delta\alpha\!\sim\!4.7\times10^{-8}$~\cite{RiembauTeraZ});
\item \code{emela\_ll=True} --- eMELA's DGLAP-evolved BETA-scheme
  \emph{LL} ePDF, used as a diagnostic to isolate the LL-truncation
  error of the analytic $\beta^3$ Beenakker formula
  (eq.~\eqref{eq:beenakker-beta}) from the pure NLL bracket.
\end{itemize}
Card knobs: \code{NLO\_CONFIG["isr\_nll"]} is \code{True} (the production
default since 2026-05-29 --- eMELA NLL) and
\code{NLO\_CONFIG["isr\_emela\_ll"]} is \code{False}; the LL$+$exp
single-convolution baseline (\code{isr\_nll=False}) is retained only for
the BFS-table closure reruns.
\code{isr\_emela\_\{pert\_order,fac\_scheme,ren\_scheme\}}
expose the eMELA scheme. The production
\code{ren\_scheme="ALPMZ"} default was switched from the BFS-prescription
\code{"ALGMU"} on 2026-05-28: BFS's hard $\hat\sigma$ and the ISR
$\beta_e$ are formally independent at NLL, the collinear-finite
$c^{(1,\rm fin)}$ carries no $\mu_F$-cancellation against $D(x,Q)$ in
DELTA, and $\alpha(M_Z)$ is the directly-measured input.
The genuine NLL theory uncertainty
lives in the renormalisation-scheme variation
ALPMZ$\leftrightarrow$ALGMU$\leftrightarrow\alpha(0)$,
while the $\alpha(M_Z)$ \emph{input} uncertainty is the separate profiled
$\alpha_{\rm em,ISR}$ nuisance with $\Delta\alpha(M_Z)=4.7\times 10^{-8}$ (FCC-ee
combined Tera-Z~\cite{RiembauTeraZ}); eMELA returns $\sim 1.6\,\%$ larger
$\sigma$ at the WW threshold under ALPMZ$+\alpha(M_Z)$ than under the
legacy ALGMU$+\alpha_{G_\mu}$ choice, so the global cross-section
normalisation shifts but the off-diagonal LL$\leftrightarrow$NLL
biases in section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross} are unchanged at the
$\sim\!1\MeV$ level. An ISR factorisation-scale variation
($\xi\!\in\![0.5,2]$ on $Q=\xi\sqrt{s}$) is implemented in \code{isr.py}
for legacy use but not exposed as a card knob: in DELTA + finite
$c^{(1,\rm fin)}$ the variation measures DGLAP-evolution numerics rather
than a residual NLL truncation (\code{scripts/investigations/nll\_isr/scale\_decouple\_diagnostic.py}). The two flags are mutually exclusive (runtime
\code{ValueError}). Quadrature defaults are
$z_\text{min}=0.30$ throughout, with $n_\text{quad}=128$ per leg on the
2-leg path (NLL, eMELA-LL, and the BFS-reference 2-leg single-leg
diagnostic) and $n_\text{quad}=200$ on the production LL$+$exp
single-convolution 1D quadrature --- picked by a dedicated convergence
study (max relative residual $\sim\!2\times 10^{-4}$ at WW threshold on
the 2-leg path, scheme-independent); tighter $z_\text{min}$ wastes
Gauss-Legendre nodes on the empty region below the kinematic threshold
$z_{\rm kin}\!\approx\!0.985$ where $\hat\sigma$ vanishes. The size of the residual $\mW$ bias from
staying at LL$+$exp instead of moving to NLL is quantified directly in
section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross}.

\paragraph{Numerical cost and radiator caching.}
The eMELA NLL ePDF is the runtime bottleneck of the chain: each
\code{code_pdf}$(x,1{-}x,Q)$ call re-evolves DGLAP in Mellin space
($\sim\!22\,$ms), and a single morph/template build issues
$\mathcal{O}(10^4)$ such queries. Crucially the per-leg radiator weight
$D(x)\,|\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}u|$ depends only on $(\sqrts,\ \text{ISR
configuration})$ and \emph{not} on the partonic $\hat\sigma$, so it is
identical across the entire $\{\text{LO, NLO, NNLO},\,\delta_\text{QCD}\}$
ladder and across every $(\mW,\GammaW)$ morph point. We therefore cache
the $\hat\sigma$-independent radiator in two layers --- an in-process
table and an on-disk store (\code{$WW_ISR_RADIATOR_CACHE}) shared
across processes and AFS nodes --- byte-identical to a fresh build and
gated to the eMELA paths only (the NLL \code{code_pdf} and the
diagnostic eMELA-LL \code{ll_pdf}; the analytic LL$+$exp form of
eq.~\eqref{eq:beenakker-beta} is a fast closed form and is never cached).
A \code{prewarm} helper builds the disk store once, up front, so a
cross-node \texttt{condor} fan-out only ever \emph{reads} it rather than
each job racing to rebuild identical arrays (AFS provides no reliable
cross-node lock); the build is keyed per-$\sqrts$ on the BFS production
chain (\code{xsec_calculator/isr.py}, whose internal parallelism is over
$\sqrts$) and per-grid on the independent MoCaNLO chain
(\code{indep/isr_beta.py}), which additionally pre-warms the radiator
before its varpoint fork-pool by default. The caching is purely
computational --- it changes no cross section (verified byte-identical) ---
and shortens a morph build by $\times4$--$5$ on the BFS chain and from
$27$ to $2\,$s on the independent chain. As an alternative the
independent chain can replace the DGLAP solver entirely by interpolating
eMELA off a precomputed $(1{-}x,\,Q)$ grid (stored in the LHAPDF
\texttt{lhagrid1} format): laid dense toward the soft endpoint
$x\!\to\!1$ where ISR lives and interpolating $\ln(xD)$ in
$(\ln(1{-}x),\,\ln Q)$, it reproduces the fitted $\mW$ to
$\Delta\mW\!=\!0.0\,$MeV while removing the runtime eMELA dependency.

the paper's 1-decimal rounding floor at every $\sqrts$ in the scan
window. Achieving Table~2 closure at this level required the correct
BR-correction prescription for the energy-dependent
$\sigma^{(3/2),a}$ piece: it is a next-to-leading order in $E$ of the
\emph{hard}-region contribution that starts at $\sigma^{(1/2)}$ (same
h1--h3 cut diagrams of Fig.~6 of \cite{BFSnlo}, next order in the
threshold expansion). The flavour-specific BR correction derived in
paper~\S6.1 for the hard region therefore applies a \emph{linear}
prefactor $\GammaWzero/\GammaW$ to $\sigma^{(3/2),a}$, not the squared
$(\GammaWzero/\GammaW)^2$ used for the potential-region pieces
$\sigma^{(0)}$ and $\sigma^{(1)}_{\mathrm{pot}}$. With the squared
factor the closure carried a clean
$-2.3\,\%\cdot\sigma^{(3/2),a}/\sigma_\text{tot}$ residual, growing
$\propto E$ to $-0.06\,\%$ at $170\GeV$. The linear factor closes both
the $\sim 0.9\,\%$ sub-threshold tail and the $\propto E$
above-threshold slope simultaneously.

\section{Sensitivity and uncertainty estimates from the fit}\label{sec:fitresults}

The preceding section established that the cross-section chain reproduces

\subsection{Selection efficiency and cross-section systematics}\label{sec:fitresults-expsyst}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The headline fit treats the reconstructed cross section as an ideal counting
measurement. Two production refinements bring it closer to a real analysis;
both are placeholders pending a full reconstruction study and both are steered
from \code{cards/ww_default.py}.

\paragraph{Selection and reconstruction efficiency.}
A measured (efficiency-corrected) cross section is reconstructed from
$N_\text{sel}=\varepsilon\,\sigma\,\mathcal{L}$ selected events, so its
per-point statistical uncertainty grows by $1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ while the
central value is unchanged. We adopt a placeholder $\varepsilon=0.8$
(\code{SCENARIO["selection_efficiency"]}, applied in
\code{FitCore.create_scenario}), which inflates every statistical term by
$1/\sqrt{0.8}\approx1.118$. This replaces the WbWb-style flat $1.2$ stat
inflation (used there for background contamination, never for WW) with a
physically-motivated knob; $\varepsilon=1$ recovers the ideal case and is the
default for any card that does not set it.

\paragraph{Placeholder cross-section systematics.}
We add two systematic uncertainties on the measured $\sigma(\sqrts)$, each sized
as $30\%$ of the per-point statistical uncertainty $\sigma^\text{stat}_i$: one
\emph{fully correlated} across the $\sqrts$ points and one \emph{fully
uncorrelated}. They enter the data covariance directly,
\begin{equation*}
  C \;=\; C^\text{stat}
  \;+\; \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl[(0.3\,\sigma^\text{stat}_i)^2\bigr]
  \;+\; (0.3\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^\text{stat})\,(0.3\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^\text{stat})^{\!\top},
\end{equation*}
where the rank-one outer product is the fully-correlated piece
(\code{card.XSEC_SYST}, activated for the production fit via
\code{FitCore.set_xsec_systematics}; the theory-ladder, scenario-comparison,
channel-extrapolation and independent-MoCaNLO fits deliberately do not activate
them). The resulting Asimov breakdown on the baseline $7$-point scan is in
Table~\ref{tab:expsyst-budget}: the two cross-section systematics together
contribute $\sim\!0.6\MeV$ to $\sigma(\mW)$ and $\sim\!0.9\MeV$ to
$\sigma(\GammaW)$, and dominate the experimental systematic budget at this
(placeholder) size.

\paragraph{Statistical correlation between the $\sqrts$ points.}
A real measurement can induce statistical correlations between the cross
sections at different $\sqrts$ (shared control samples, common unfolding,
overlapping selections). Figure~\ref{fig:statcorr} sweeps a single global
point-to-point statistical correlation $\rho$, with
$C^\text{stat}_{ij}=\rho\,\sigma^\text{stat}_i\,\sigma^\text{stat}_j$ for
$i\neq j$, over $\rho\in[0,0.99]$ (statistics only, all systematics off;
\code{doFit_ww.py --statCorrScan}). Counter-intuitively the sensitivities
\emph{improve} with $\rho$: a common statistical mode cancels in the line
shape, so the point-to-point differences that fix $(\mW,\GammaW)$ are pinned
ever more tightly (their inverse-covariance weight $\propto 1/(1-\rho)$) while
only the common-normalisation mode loses information. $\sigma(\GammaW)$ falls
monotonically; $\sigma(\mW)$ first rises slightly to $\rho\!\approx\!0.45$ ---
its residual rate handle is degraded by the common mode --- then falls. The
$\rho\!\to\!1$ fully-correlated limit is singular (rank-one covariance) and is
approached but not reached.

\subsection{ISR-scheme systematic on the fitted $m_W$}\label{sec:val-isr-cross}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The eMELA NLL plumbing (section~\ref{sec:isr}) lets us measure
\emph{directly} the residual ISR systematic on $\mW$ that was estimated
by BFS at $\delta\mW\!\approx\!31\MeV$ on theoretical grounds. Two
fit-level checks make the case: (i) does the linear template-morph
close under each ISR scheme, and (ii) what is the $\mW$ bias from
mismatching the templates' ISR scheme to the truth?

\paragraph{Morph closure post-ISR.}
For each of the three ISR backends --- analytic LL$+$exp,
eMELA LL (DGLAP), eMELA NLL (DELTA$+$ALPMZ) --- we evaluate
$\sigma_\text{obs}(\sqrts; \mW^0,\GammaW^0)$,
$\sigma_\text{obs}(\sqrts; \mW^0+\delta m,\GammaW^0)$ and
$\sigma_\text{obs}(\sqrts; \mW^0+\delta m/2,\GammaW^0)$ on the FCC-ee

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
At the FCC-ee statistical target $\sigma(\mW)\!=\!0.25\MeV$ (FSR
Vol.~1 Table~2,~\cite{FCCFSR2025}), the analytic LL$+$exp ISR bias of
$\pm 22\MeV$ would be $\sim 90\times$ the statistical floor.
Production fits at the precision target must run with
\code{isr\_nll=True}. Switching to eMELA's DGLAP LL alone
(\code{isr\_emela\_ll=True}) closes the LL-truncation piece but leaves
a $\pm 5\MeV$ residual from the unresummed NLL bracket --- useful as a
diagnostic but not a viable production fallback. The
BFS-quoted $\delta\mW\!\approx\!31\MeV$~\cite{BFSnlo} is a conservative
upper bound on this systematic; our direct measurement gives a tighter
$22\MeV$ value with the LL$+$exp recipe specified above.

\subsection{Incremental theory-piece ladder}\label{sec:val-theory-ladder}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The chain-routing cross-fit above (section~\ref{sec:val-chain-cross})
compares the production chain against \emph{historical} alternatives.
This subsection instead turns the BFS perturbative pieces on one at a
time and measures how each successive correction moves the fitted
$(\mW,\GammaW)$ --- a first, internally-consistent estimate of the
theory uncertainty carried by the truncation of the calculation itself.
This is a standalone mode of the main fit driver,
\code{doFit\_ww.py --theoryLadder} (orchestration in
\code{framework/process/ww/theory\_ladder.py}).

\paragraph{Ladder and methodology.}
Four cumulative rungs are built on a \emph{fixed} WHIZARD-Born anchor
(the anchor is not a perturbative order; it is the common Born the whole
series rides on):
\begin{equation*}
  \text{LO}\;\to\;\text{+NLO loops}\;\to\;\text{+NNLO}\;\to\;\text{+}\delta_\text{QCD},
\end{equation*}
each run with both LL$+$exp and NLL ISR ($8$ chain configurations). For
every configuration an Asimov fit injects the \emph{full production
chain} ($+\delta_\text{QCD}$ with NLL ISR) as truth and lets Minuit find
$(\mW,\GammaW)$; the best-fit shift away from the injected truth is the
residual theory bias of stopping at that rung. To isolate a genuine
theory effect from the trivial normalisation difference between
perturbative orders, the fit floats $\mW$ and $\GammaW$ only, with
\emph{statistical $+$ correlated-luminosity} uncertainties and nothing
else ($\alpha_s$, BEC, BES, $m_t$, $M_H$, $M_Z$ all fixed). Each rung is
fit twice:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{free} --- the correlated-luminosity prior is opened up
  ($\sim\!100\,\%$), so any flat $\sigma$-rescale between orders is fully
  absorbed by the normalisation and the residual is a \emph{pure shape}
  bias on $\mW$;
  \item \textbf{prior} --- the realistic FCC-ee correlated-lumi prior
  ($1\times10^{-4}$, the di-photon corr.\ component), so the flat component
  that leaks into $\mW$ under the true constraint is included.
\end{itemize}
Both modes use the threshold scan $\sqrts\in[157,163]\GeV$, no
above-threshold point; the per-rung $\sigma_{\mW}$ here is therefore a
test-specific figure (2-POI, stat$+$corr-lumi only), not the headline
analysis sensitivity.

  $\sim\!2.3\MeV$ (free) / $\sim\!3.4\MeV$ (prior). Taking the size of

  through the (here-disabled) $\alpha_s$ nuisance. This is the same
  mechanism that made the $\delta_\text{QCD}$-routing cross-fit of
  section~\ref{sec:val-chain-cross} a pure normalisation effect.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{ISR scheme.}
The LL$\to$NLL difference at the converged ($+$NNLO/$+\delta_\text{QCD}$)
rung is the residual ISR-scheme bias on $\mW$: $+2.1\MeV$ in free mode
(pure shape) and $-6.5\MeV$ under the realistic lumi prior, with
$|\Delta\GammaW|\!\lesssim\!1\MeV$. This is consistent in magnitude with
the $\pm5.4\MeV$ pure-NLL figure of the dedicated ISR cross-fit
(section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross}) and reaffirms that running the analysis
with the NLL default (\code{isr_nll=True}) is necessary at the FCC-ee
$0.25\MeV$ target.

\paragraph{Free vs.\ prior.}
The two lumi modes bracket the role of the absolute rate. With the
normalisation floated (\textbf{free}) the residuals are smaller in
central value but the stat precision degrades to
$\sigma_{\mW}\!\approx\!4.7\MeV$ (the rate carries $\mW$ information that
is thrown away); with the tight realistic prior (\textbf{prior}) the rate
sharpens $\sigma_{\mW}$ to $\approx\!1.31\MeV$ but couples a low-order
normalisation error into $\mW$ --- which is why the LO bias balloons to
$\sim\!90$--$100\MeV$ and the NNLO residual grows from $2.3$ to $3.4\MeV$. The
honest perturbative-truncation number for the budget is the
\textbf{free} (pure-shape) NNLO step, $\sim\!2.3\MeV$; the prior column
shows how much a low-order rate error would additionally leak through the
luminosity constraint.

\paragraph{What this ladder does \emph{not} capture.}
By construction the ladder only bounds the missing \emph{higher order of
pieces already in the chain}. Two genuine theory uncertainties live
entirely outside it and need a separate, non-ladder estimate:
(i) NLO electroweak corrections of the YFSWW3 class (non-factorisable
photon exchange and initial--final interference), absent from the
BFS-LO$_\text{EFT}$ Born --- the structural part of the residual gap to
the Azzurri~\cite{Azzurri2021} $0.5\MeV$ projection
(section~\ref{sec:val-chain-cross}); and (ii) higher-order Coulomb,
i.e.\ the BFS unstable-$W$ Green function $G_C$ versus the dropped FKM
$K_C$ multiplier (sections~\ref{sec:kcsafe}, \ref{sec:limits}). These set
the priority for the next round of theory-uncertainty work.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{ISR scheme-variation uncertainty}\label{sec:val-isr-scheme}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The perturbative ladder above estimates the missing higher order by the
size of the last included term --- the standard proxy when a true scheme
variation is unavailable. On the \emph{hard} (BFS) side it has to be:
the calculation lives in a fixed electroweak input scheme ($G_\mu$
coupling, on-shell $\sin^2\theta_W=1-\mW^2/M_Z^2$) baked into the analytic
NLO matching coefficients $c^{(1,\text{fin})}$ (section~\ref{sec:nlo}),
so a genuine EW-scheme variation would mean re-deriving the entire
matching tower --- a separate, multi-week project, out of scope for the
match-to-BFS goal. The \emph{ISR} side, by contrast, admits a clean
scheme variation that is fully decoupled from the BFS $\hat\sigma$: the
hard cross section and the ISR exponent $\beta_e$ are formally independent
at NLL, so changing the eMELA renormalisation scheme of the running QED
coupling needs no change to $c^{(1,\text{fin})}$. This is run as part of
the same driver (\code{--theoryLadder}); it rides on the full production
hard side ($+\delta_\text{QCD}$) with NLL ISR throughout, varying only the
ISR knobs and fitting each against the production truth (ALPMZ, $\xi=1$).

\paragraph{Knobs varied.}
Two handles, with very different status:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{$\alpha$ renormalisation scheme} (the headline). The
  coupling in $\beta_e=(2\alpha/\pi)(\ln(s/m_e^2)-1)$ is taken in three
  schemes: \code{ALPMZ} ($\alpha(M_Z)=1/128.94$, production --- what
  FCC-ee measures via off-peak $A_\text{FB}^{\mu\mu}$), \code{ALGMU}
  ($\alpha_{G_\mu}\!\approx\!1/132.17$, the BFS prescription), and
  \code{FIXED}/$\alpha(0)$ ($1/137.04$, Thomson). Since $\beta_e\propto
  \alpha$, the $2.5\,\%$ (ALPMZ$\to$ALGMU) to $5.9\,\%$
  (ALPMZ$\to\alpha(0)$) coupling spread is a genuine NLL-order (formally
  N$^2$LL) ambiguity that shifts the threshold lineshape.
  \item \textbf{Factorisation scale} $\xi$ ($Q=\xi\sqrt s$,
  $\xi\in\{0.5,2\}$), reported as a \emph{DGLAP-stability check only}.
  In the DELTA scheme with a collinear-finite $c^{(1,\text{fin})}$ the
  $\hat\sigma$ carries no $\mu_F$ dependence to cancel the evolution of
  the structure function, so $\xi$-variation probes the numerical
  stability of the eMELA evolution, not a physical truncation
  uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reading the table (Table~\ref{tab:isr-scheme}).}
The pure-shape (\textbf{free}) column is modest: the ISR coupling-scheme
choice moves $\mW$ by $+3.5\MeV$ (ALPMZ$\to$ALGMU) to $+5.8\MeV$
(ALPMZ$\to\alpha(0)$) once the rate is floated. The realistic-prior
column is far larger --- $+38\MeV$ (ALGMU) and $+79\MeV$ ($\alpha(0)$)
--- because a $2.5$--$5.9\,\%$ change in the ISR coupling rescales the
\emph{total} radiated energy, and that normalisation shift is \emph{not}
absorbable by a luminosity constrained to $1\times10^{-4}$, so it leaks
directly into $\mW$. The $\xi$ rows behave the same way ($\pm45\MeV$
under the prior, near-cancelling in free mode), confirming they are a
normalisation-driven stability artefact rather than a clean uncertainty
--- exactly the reason $\xi$ was removed from the production card.

\paragraph{Cross-section-level verification.}
Because the prior-mode shifts are large, the effect was checked
independently at the $\sigma$ level (script
\code{scripts/investigations/isr\_scheme/verify\_isr\_alpha\_scheme.py}).
The ratio $R(\sqrts)=\sigma_\text{scheme}/\sigma_\text{ALPMZ}$ on
$\sqrts\in[157,163]\GeV$ is an almost \emph{flat} offset: $R-1=+1.62\,\%$
(ALGMU) and $+3.43\,\%$ ($\alpha(0)$), with a $\sqrts$-slope of only
$\sim\!10^{-3}\,\%/\GeV$ --- i.e.\ the shape distortion sits at or below
the quadrature floor, and the mean offset is stable to $2.5\times10^{-5}$
across $n_\text{quad}=128/192/256$. Three controls confirm the effect is
genuine and purely from ISR: (i) the \emph{partonic} $\hat\sigma$ is
identical across the three schemes (the ISR coupling never touches the
hard side); (ii) the shift tracks the $\alpha$ \emph{value} entering
$\beta_e$ (about half) plus the eMELA DGLAP renormalisation-scheme running
(about half), not the scheme \emph{string}; (iii) $\beta_e$ scales exactly
linearly in $\alpha$. So the scheme dependence is a nearly-pure
\emph{normalisation} shift of the ISR-corrected rate: with the rate floated
(free) only the tiny slope biases $\mW$ (the residual few-MeV figure is
mostly the statistically-constrained rate, not a clean shape), while under
the tight luminosity prior the $1.6$--$3.4\,\%$ rate offset cannot be
absorbed and is compensated by $\mW$ sliding along the steep threshold
turn-on ($\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\sqrts\!\approx\!+0.16\,\text{pb}/\GeV$), giving the
$\sim\!38$/$79\MeV$ shifts --- the factor $\sim\!10$ between prior and free
is exactly the dominant-normalisation expectation.

\paragraph{Interpretation and recommended number.}
ALPMZ is the theoretically preferred scheme: the NLL resummation is built
consistently with the measured $\alpha(M_Z)$, and $\alpha(0)$ is a known
\emph{poor} choice for a high-scale collinear process (it omits the large
QED running logs the NLL evolution is designed to capture), so the
$\alpha(0)$ row is an extreme bound rather than an equal-validity
alternative. Taking the ALPMZ$\leftrightarrow$ALGMU spread as the
defensible scheme variation gives a residual ISR theory uncertainty of
$\approx\!3.5\MeV$ (pure shape) rising to $\approx\!38\MeV$ once the
non-absorbable rate component is included under the realistic lumi
constraint. Either way the message is the same as the LL$\to$NLL
cross-fit (section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross}): \textbf{ISR is the dominant
single theory systematic on $\mW$}, an order of magnitude or more above
the $\mathcal{O}(2$--$3\MeV)$ perturbative-truncation proxy, and well
above the $0.25\MeV$ FCC-ee target. Pinning the ISR coupling scheme (and
ultimately the input $\alpha(M_Z)$ to the Tera-$Z$ precision) is
therefore a precondition for an MeV-level $\mW$ extraction.

\paragraph{Factorisation scheme: DELTA is the matched choice.}
The DELTA$\leftrightarrow$MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ factorisation question has been
studied directly (item~\ref{item:scheme-match}, section~\ref{sec:match-todo}) and
DELTA confirmed as the matched scheme. DELTA is the scheme consistent with
$\hat\sigma=\sigma_\text{Born}$ (no ISR collinear subtraction) --- the QED DIS-like
scheme, in which the finite initial-state collinear remainder is absorbed into the
PDF~\cite{eMELAdelta} --- so eMELA's DELTA radiator equals the BFS LL subtractant by
construction (section~\ref{sec:isr}). MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ differs by the
closed-form finite kernel
$D_{\overline{\rm MS}}\!-\!D_\Delta=-(\alpha/2\pi)\,[(1{+}x^2)/(1{-}x)\,(2\ln(1{-}x){+}1)]_+$,
which would have to be added to $\hat\sigma$ as a collinear counterterm; convolving the
MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ ePDF without it double-counts that term and roughly doubles the
near-threshold line shape. An $\alpha$-stability scan confirms DELTA is $\alpha$-stable
(the unmatched MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ term, being $\propto\alpha$, is absent), so a
DELTA$-$MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ spread is not a residual ISR-scheme uncertainty here.

\paragraph{Findings.}
In the geometry-isolating 2-POI view (Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}) the
3-point layout loses very little on $\mW$ relative to the 7-point (the
threshold turn-on is anchored by the end points), at a small cost in
$\GammaW$. The Azzurri-like 2-point layout \emph{decorrelates} the two POIs
--- $\rho$ drops from $+0.66$ (7-point) to $+0.41$ --- and gives the tightest
$\GammaW$, the upper point near the $\GammaW$-insensitive crossing carrying
almost pure $\mW$ information. It does not reach the $\rho\!\approx\!0$
Azzurri quotes, because the correlated-luminosity-only constraint used here
leaves a residual coupling. (The pure-shape ``free'' fit is degenerate for
the 2-point layout --- two points cannot constrain two POIs plus a floating
normalisation --- so only the realistic-prior column is meaningful there.)

The full systematics breakdown (Table~\ref{tab:scenario-syst}) tells the
operationally important story: with the \emph{realistic} FCC-ee priors --- in
particular the di-photon luminosity prior ($2\!\times\!10^{-4}$ uncorrelated,
per-point scaled, section~\ref{sec:val-scenarios}), an order of magnitude tighter
than the earlier placeholder --- the $\mW$ budget is \textbf{statistics-dominated}
and \textbf{almost layout-independent}. The total experimental $\sigma(\mW)$ is
$1.36$--$1.40\MeV$ across all three geometries; the luminosity contributes only
$0.37$--$0.41\MeV$ and the leading systematic is the beam-energy calibration
(BEC, $\approx0.2\MeV$), with $\alpha_s$, $\alpha_{\rm em,ISR}$,
$\alpha_{\rm em,EW}$ and BES all $\lesssim\!0.1\MeV$. The scan geometry therefore moves the budget only through
(i) the statistical term --- $\sigma_\text{stat}(\mW)$ grows mildly from
$0.95\MeV$ (7-point) to $1.18\MeV$ (Azzurri-like) as points are removed --- and
(ii) the luminosity--$\GammaW$ coupling: the Azzurri-like upper point near the
$\GammaW$-insensitive crossing decorrelates the lumi nuisance from $\GammaW$,
giving it both the smallest lumi entry on $\GammaW$ ($0.33$ vs.\ $0.48\MeV$) and
the tightest $\sigma(\GammaW)$ ($2.30\MeV$). The multi-point scan remains the
robust default for $\mW$; the realistic-prior budget shows no penalty for the
2-point layout on $\mW$ (in contrast to the earlier placeholder-prior analysis
where the under-constrained luminosity made the ranking invert), so the choice
between layouts can be driven by $\GammaW$ and by robustness margins rather than
by $\mW$ exposure to the luminosity systematic.

\paragraph{Rate vs.\ shape: the shape-only fit.}
The luminosity dependence above poses a sharper question: how much of the $\mW$
precision is carried by the \emph{absolute rate} (the overall normalisation) and
how much by the \emph{shape} of the threshold turn-on? A \emph{shape-only} fit
answers it directly (\code{doFit\_ww.py --shapeOnly}): the correlated luminosity
nuisance is floated to $\approx\!100\%$ (its prior opened so any overall
normalisation is fully absorbed) and the uncorrelated per-point luminosity is
switched off, while every other systematic keeps its production prior --- so only
the normalisation constraint changes relative to the headline fit.

\paragraph{Stability of $\sigma$ and $\rho$ across the ladder.}
A consistency requirement on the whole exercise is that the
\emph{uncertainties} and the $\mW$--$\GammaW$ \emph{correlation} be set by the
lineshape and the luminosity constraint, \emph{not} by which perturbative
pieces happen to be switched on: the perturbative order moves the central
value (the bias of Table~\ref{tab:theory-ladder}), it should not move the
error ellipse. Table~\ref{tab:scenario-stability} confirms this for every
scan layout. Across the four ladder rungs $\sigma_{\mW}$ varies by
$\le\!0.01\MeV$, $\sigma_{\GammaW}$ by $\le\!0.03\MeV$ and $\rho$ by

(the LO Born lineshape is slightly too shallow), after which all three are
flat to the rounding. The error ellipse is therefore a property of the scan
geometry alone, which is exactly why the per-rung bias (not its error bar) is
the meaningful theory-uncertainty figure, and why the geometry comparison of
Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} is rung-independent.

\subsection{Channel extrapolation: $\mu\nu q\bar q\to$ inclusive WW}\label{sec:val-channel-extrap}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

All fits above are performed in the reconstructed $\mu\nu q\bar q$ channel.
The threshold lineshape $\sigma(\sqrts)$ is the same for every WW decay
channel --- in the \code{pdg-constant} convention the branching ratio is a
flat multiplicative factor that cancels in the fit's relative response ---
so moving to the full inclusive WW sample multiplies the event yield by
$1/B_{\mu\nu q\bar q}$ with $B_{\mu\nu q\bar q}=2\,\mathrm{BR}(W\!\to\!\mu\nu)\,
\mathrm{BR}(W\!\to\!\text{had})\approx0.143$ (a factor $\approx7$), and
changes nothing else about the \emph{experimental} fit. The inclusive sample
has $1/B$ times more events at the \emph{same} machine luminosity, so its
statistical uncertainty shrinks by $\sqrt{B}$ while every relative systematic
--- and crucially the luminosity measurement itself (the di-photon/Bhabha
count of the real machine luminosity, identical for any WW final state) ---
is unchanged. We therefore implement the boost as a \textbf{statistical-only
rescale} (the stat term $\times\sqrt{B}$ at the real luminosity;
\code{doFit\_ww.py --channelExtrap}), \emph{not} as a luminosity inflation
$L\to L/B$, which would wrongly shrink the per-point luminosity-counting prior
(section~\ref{sec:val-scenarios}). The full Asimov fit then maps every relative
systematic through correctly --- the only honest way to extrapolate, since a
naive $\sqrt{B}$ scaling of the \emph{total} uncertainty would also shrink the
irreducible systematics. \textbf{Theory uncertainties are deliberately not
extrapolated} here: they differ channel-by-channel and are the
$\mu\nu q\bar q$-specific numbers of the preceding subsections.

\section{Independent BFS-free cross-check: MoCaNLO\,+\,Recola NLO-EW}\label{sec:indep}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\emph{The inclusive ($\mu\nu qq$-shape) pure-WW pipeline, the independent
sensitivities (with the channel scope matched to BFS), the fiducial-acceptance
cross-check, the ISR- and EW-input-scheme theory uncertainties, the full
$157$--$365\GeV$ cross section, and a $240\GeV$ luminosity anchor are all complete
and reported below, on a grid synchronised to the production inputs
($m_t=172.5\GeV$, $M_H=125.25\GeV$, $M_Z=91.1876\GeV$,
$G_F=1.1663787\times10^{-5}$).}

\subsection{Motivation and scope}\label{sec:indep-scope}

The entire chain validated in section~\ref{sec:val} rests on the BFS
unstable-particle EFT. As an \emph{independent} check of the extracted
sensitivities --- not a number-by-number reproduction --- we have built a
completely BFS-free line-shape calculation that shares no code and no
perturbative input with the EFT chain, plugged into the \emph{same}
\code{WWGenerator} fit contract (section~\ref{sec:codeorg}). The goal is to
answer ``does an independent NLO-EW four-fermion calculation give the same
$\sigma(\mW)$, $\sigma(\GammaW)$ and $\rho$?''. This closes the
``parallel calculation'' open item flagged for publication.

\subsection{The calculation}\label{sec:indep-calc}

The partonic cross section $\hat\sigma(\sqrt{\hat s})$ for each four-fermion
final state is computed at NLO~EW with \textbf{MoCaNLO}~\cite{MoCaNLO} (release
1.0.0) driving \textbf{Recola1}~\cite{Recola1} (1.5.1) and
\textbf{Collier}~\cite{Collier} (1.2.9) for the one-loop amplitudes, in the
complex-mass scheme with the full off-shell $e^+e^-\!\to 4f$ matrix element (no
double-pole approximation), so the on-shell $\mW$ enters as the project POI and
is converted internally to the pole mass. Two toolchain validations anchor the
setup: $e^+e^-\!\to t\bar t$ Born agrees with the shipped reference to
$0.4\,\sigma$, and $e^+e^-\!\to\mu^-\bar\nu_\mu u\bar d$ Born at $\sqrt s=161\GeV$
($157.4\fb$) matches the WHIZARD grid once the $-27\MeV$ on-shell$\to$pole
conversion is applied, to $0.07\%$.

\paragraph{Electroweak inputs.} The SM inputs are synchronised to the production
card --- $M_Z=91.1876\GeV$, $m_t=172.5\GeV$, $M_H=125.25\GeV$,
$G_F=1.1663787\times10^{-5}\GeV^{-2}$ --- so the independent calculation and the
BFS chain evaluate at the same electroweak point. In the $G_\mu$ scheme $\mW$ is
a fixed input and the radiative correction $\Delta r$ is absorbed into the
$G_F$--$\mW$ relation, so the residual sensitivity of $\hat\sigma$ to $m_t$ is
strongly screened: regenerating the grid at the older
$(m_t,M_H)=(174.2,125.0)\GeV$ point shifts the NLO cross section by only
$-0.0011\%$, far below the per-point Monte-Carlo error. The sensitivities below
are therefore independent of this input choice at the relevant precision.

Initial-state radiation is supplied by a \emph{decoupled} beta-scheme LL
radiator implemented independently in Python (\code{framework/process/ww/indep/
isr\_beta.py}), following the collinear-factorisation construction
of~\cite{ISRcollinear} and convolved by Gauss--Legendre quadrature,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\text{obs}(s)=\iint D(x_1)\,D(x_2)\,
  \hat\sigma_\text{NLO}(x_1x_2\,s)\;\mathrm dx_1\,\mathrm dx_2,
  \label{eq:indep-obs}
\end{equation}
with \emph{no} $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ subtraction: the radiator alone carries the
initial-state radiation. Keeping the ISR factorised from the partonic generator
lets the ISR scheme be varied (section~\ref{sec:indep-isr}) without regenerating
the NLO-EW grid.

\paragraph{No $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ re-subtraction (2026-06-12 correction).}
The original implementation subtracted the $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ radiator overlap
$C_1[\hat\sigma_\text{Born}]$ from equation~\eqref{eq:indep-obs}, on the premise
that the \code{pdf\_set=none} grids were mass-regularised fixed-order results
carrying the explicit $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ ISR logarithm $\ln(\hat s/m_e^2)$.
The production grids show that premise to be false: MoCaNLO's integrated-dipole
run applies the initial-state collinear counterterm, so the ISR logarithm is
factorised \emph{out} of $\hat\sigma_\text{NLO}$. At $\sqrts=163\GeV$ (lnuqq)
the real-emission and integrated-dipole contributions are $+4.2\%$ and $+1.7\%$
of Born, $\hat\sigma_\text{NLO}/\hat\sigma_\text{Born}=+8.3\%$ --- no trace of
the ${\approx}{-}30\%$ radiative tail --- and $\hat\sigma_\text{NLO}$ agrees with
the ISR-free BFS partonic to $1.2\%$. Subtracting $C_1$ therefore \emph{added
back} the physical ISR damping and inflated $\sigma_\text{obs}$ by
${\approx}{+}48\%$: the chain gave $\sigma_{WW}(161.3\GeV)\approx5.4$\,pb against
the LEP measurement $3.69\pm0.45$\,pb~\cite{LEP2EW}. With the correction it
gives $3.52$\,pb there and $5.41$\,pb at $163\GeV$, consistent with the BFS
chain ($3.45$/$5.30$\,pb) and with YFSWW3 (${\approx}5.5$\,pb at $163\GeV$, read
from the $\sigma_{WW}(\sqrts)$ curve of~\cite{Azzurri2021}); see
table~\ref{tab:threecalc-163}. The $C_1$ kernel is retained as a diagnostic
(its $\mathcal O(\alpha^2)$ exactness against the convolution remains a unit
test); it would re-enter the observable only for grids that genuinely carry the
mass-regularised $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ ISR logarithm. \emph{The independent-fit
numbers in this section were produced before this correction: the absolute rate
was uniformly $\times1.48$ too high, so the quoted statistical uncertainties
are ${\approx}\sqrt{1.48}=1.22$ times too small; shape-level results are
affected only through the mild $\sqrts$-dependence of the damping. A re-fit on
the corrected chain is pending.}

\paragraph{Channel set.} The leptonic forward-electron ($t$-channel single-$W$)
and same-flavour ($\gamma^\ast\!\to\ell\ell$) configurations drive
small-Gram-determinant instabilities in the one-loop reduction; the inclusive
``pure-WW'' definition therefore sums the doubly-resonant blocks
$\sigma_{\rm tot}=12\cdot\ell\nu qq + 4\cdot qqqq + 9\cdot\mu\tau$
(colour$\times$flavour sum rule $(2N_c+3)^2=81$), using a mixed non-electron
leptonic representative ($\mu\tau$) for the leptonic shape. The fiducial
acceptance definition --- $|\cos\theta_\ell|<0.97$, $p_{T,\ell}>10\GeV$, and
$m_{\ell\ell}>10\GeV$ on same-flavour opposite-sign pairs --- tames the two
instabilities (the $m_{\ell\ell}$ leg removes the $\gamma^\ast\!\to\ell\ell$
low-mass pole) and restores the electron and same-flavour channels; it is being
generated separately as an acceptance cross-check. The angular cut sits inside
the FCC-ee tracker acceptance (CLD $|\cos\theta|<0.989$, IDEA drift chamber
$\sim\!0.974$, common $150\,$mrad MDI cone) with a $97.5\%$ $\cos\theta$
efficiency on signal leptons, so $e$ and $\mu$ share the same fiducial volume.

\subsection{Template grid, de-noising and morphing}\label{sec:indep-grid}

Each $\hat\sigma$ point is integrated to $\sim\!0.2\%$ ($5\times10^5$ accepted
events) on a grid of $3$ channels $\times$ $20$ $(\mW,\GammaW)$ variation points
$\times$ $33$ values of $\sqrt{\hat s}\in[156,164]\GeV$ ($1980$ points, on
HTCondor). Two de-noising levers make the per-point Monte-Carlo error
sub-dominant to the $\sim\!0.5\%$/$10\MeV$ POI response:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correlated seeds}: the nominal and every $(\mW,\GammaW)$
    variation at a given $\sqrt{\hat s}$ share the Monte-Carlo random sequence,
    so the statistical fluctuation cancels in the morph \emph{differences}. A
    dedicated A/B test (correlated vs.\ decorrelated, $\ell\nu qq$) measures a
    \textbf{$31.9\times$} reduction of the even (curvature$+$noise) part of the
    $\pm10\MeV$ response, from RMS $0.71\%$ to $0.022\%$.
  \item \textbf{Factorized morph}: the $20$ variation points ($\mW,\GammaW$ each
    at $\pm10/\pm20/\pm30/\pm50\MeV$ plus three diagonal cross points) feed a
    factorized predictor
    \[
      \sigma(\sqrts;\mW,\GammaW)=\sigma_{\rm nom}(\sqrts)\,
        R_m(\sqrts,\Delta m)\,R_\Gamma(\sqrts,\Delta w)\,
        \bigl[1+\beta(\sqrts)\,\Delta m\,\Delta w\bigr],
    \]
    a per-$\sqrts$ quadratic ratio in each POI (fitted over its nine on-axis
    points, so the slope is averaged down rather than taken from a single finite
    difference) times a bilinear cross-term $\beta$ from the diagonal points
    (\code{framework/process/ww/indep/morph.py}). The steep line shape sits
    entirely in $\sigma_{\rm nom}$ --- reproduced \emph{exactly} --- while the
    responses $R_m,R_\Gamma\!\approx\!1$ vary slowly with $\sqrts$, isolating
    $\beta$ from the implicit $R_m R_\Gamma$ product cross.
\end{itemize}
The smoothing/morphing structure deliberately mirrors the BFS WHIZARD-grid scheme
of appendix~\ref{sec:morphval}: a weighted cubic spline in $\sqrt{\hat s}$
(weights $1/{\rm err}$) on the generator side and the same factorized
per-$\sqrts$ quadratic$+$bilinear form, then the \emph{same} fit-side
linear$+$bilinear cross-term morph (\code{CROSS\_TERMS}$=[(\rm mass,width)]$). The
morph \emph{form} is immaterial to the result: the factorized predictor and an
additive Taylor fit $[\,1,\Delta m,\Delta w,\Delta m^2,\Delta w^2,\Delta m\,\Delta
w\,]$ differ by $\le\!10^{-4}$ at the $\pm10\MeV$ template points and give
identical Asimov sensitivities, so the independent $\sigma(\mW)$, $\sigma(\GammaW)$,
$\rho$ do not hinge on the morphing choice.

Figure~\ref{fig:indep-response} overlays the independent line-shape response
$\sigma(\text{POI}\pm10\MeV)/\sigma(\text{nominal})$ on the BFS chain, per channel
and combined. The $\mW$ response agrees with BFS to $<0.1\%$ across the scan

\subsection{Independent sensitivity and the branching-ratio convention}\label{sec:indep-fit}

Running the same 2-POI covariance-luminosity Asimov fit as the theory ladder
(section~\ref{sec:val-theory-ladder}; scan $157$--$163\GeV$ step $1$,

treatment is the decisive choice:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{off-shell} (native $\sigma(4f)\propto{\rm BR}^2$): the
    $\GammaW$ rate handle is active, tightening $\sigma(\GammaW)$ and driving a
    strong \emph{anti}-correlation $\rho=-0.92$.
  \item \textbf{pdg-constant} (BFS convention): the BR is divided out
    analytically by the universal, channel-independent factor
    $f=(\GammaW/\GammaW^{0})^2(\mW^{0}/\mW)^6$ (since
    $\Gamma_{\rm partial}\propto\mW^3$), so $\GammaW$ becomes line-shape-only and
    $\rho$ returns to $+0.67$ --- matching the BFS correlation structure ---
    \emph{while retaining} the $\mW$ rate handle. The marginal $\sigma(\mW)$
    actually \emph{tightens} to $0.52\MeV$ purely by de-correlation.
  \item \textbf{shape-only} (lumi prior opened): floating the overall
    normalisation also removes the BR handle, but at the cost of the $\mW$ rate
    information, loosening $\sigma(\mW)$ to $1.76\MeV$. This is the inferior way
    to fix the BR and is reported only for contrast.
\end{itemize}
The pdg-constant column ($\sigma(\mW)=0.52\MeV$, $\rho=+0.67$) is the
recommended BFS-comparable headline: it reproduces the BFS correlation sign from
a fully independent line shape and sits below the BFS $\mW$ sensitivity
($\sim\!1.3\MeV$, section~\ref{sec:val-theory-ladder}), the MoCaNLO sum being the
full WW rate rather than the single BFS channel.

reference. Restricting the independent assembly to the same inclusive $\mu\nu qq$

\subsection{ISR-scheme theory uncertainty}\label{sec:indep-isr}

Varying the LL ISR scheme over MoCaNLO's four PDF tags
(\code{LO\_beta/eta/mixed/collinear}) leaves the \emph{sensitivity}
scheme-invariant: $\sigma(\mW)=1.15$--$1.18\MeV$ for all four (off-shell
cov-lumi), mirroring the scheme-invariance of the BFS Asimov statistics
(section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross}).
The theory uncertainty is therefore the cross-fit \emph{bias}: injecting the
reference (\code{LO\_beta}) truth line shape and fitting it with a variant
scheme's morph (\code{scripts/indep\_mocanlo/crossfit\_scheme.py}), the
physical beta-type radiators (\code{eta/mixed}) agree with the reference to
$<0.1\MeV$ ($\Delta\mW\approx0.03\MeV$ shape), while the pure-collinear PDF
(\code{LO\_collinear}, which drops the soft-resummation structure) is the
outlier at $7.2\MeV$ (shape-only) / $16\MeV$ (under the production lumi). The
conservative envelope over all four schemes is thus $\sim\!7\MeV$ on the shape;
restricting to the physical beta-type radiators it is $<0.1\MeV$. This LL-PDF-scheme
axis is distinct from the EW $\alpha$-renormalisation-scheme axis that dominates
the BFS ISR systematic (section~\ref{sec:val-isr-scheme}); the MoCaNLO analogue
of \emph{that} is the EW-input-scheme cross-fit, treated next.

\subsection{EW-input-scheme theory uncertainty}\label{sec:indep-ew}

The MoCaNLO partonic cross section is computed in the $G_\mu$ scheme by default.
Regenerating the $\hat\sigma(\sqrt{\hat s})$ grid in the $\alpha(0)$ and
$\alpha(M_Z)$ input schemes and cross-fitting against the $G_\mu$ reference truth
(same \code{crossfit\_scheme.py}) isolates the EW-input-scheme bias. Under the
production cov-lumi prior the bias is small, $|\Delta\mW|\lesssim3\MeV$
($\alpha(0)$: $-3.3\MeV$, $\alpha(M_Z)$: $+1.8\MeV$): the EW-scheme normalisation
spread on the corrected line shape is only ${\approx}1\%$ and mildly
$\sqrts$-dependent (the $G_\mu$/$\alpha(0)$ ratio runs from $-1.2\%$ at
$157.5\GeV$ to $-0.9\%$ at $162.5\GeV$), and the near-flat part is absorbed by
the luminosity nuisance. On the \emph{shape} alone (lumi free) the residual
$\sqrts$-slope of that spread maps to a larger $|\Delta\mW|\lesssim20\MeV$
($\alpha(0)$: $-11.5\MeV$, $\alpha(M_Z)$: $+19.9\MeV$), the conservative
shape-induced EW-input-scheme uncertainty. As in the BFS chain, the realistic
budget fixes the scheme (here $G_\mu$, the FCC-ee EW-fit baseline) and propagates
the residual $\alpha(M_Z)$ \emph{parametric} uncertainty rather than the full
scheme span.

\paragraph{Correction to an earlier value.} Pre-2026-06-12 drafts reported a
$\sim\!66\MeV$ cov-lumi envelope on $\mW$ here, presented as the leading theory
axis and as the MoCaNLO analogue of the BFS $\sim\!36\MeV$
$\alpha$-renormalisation-scheme systematic. That figure was an artefact of the
$C_1$ ISR subtraction since removed (section~\ref{sec:indep-calc}): because $C_1$
is $\alpha$-scheme dependent ($C_1\propto\alpha$), subtracting it injected a
spurious, scheme-dependent flat normalisation offset that the cov-lumi rate
handle amplified into the $66\MeV$ spread. With $C_1$ removed the inter-scheme
normalisation spread collapses to the ${\approx}1\%$ above, and the
EW-input-scheme is \emph{no longer} the leading theory axis under the realistic
prior. The $\alpha$-renormalisation-scheme axis of the production (BFS) chain
(section~\ref{sec:val-isr-scheme}) is unaffected and remains the dominant
ISR-related theory uncertainty of the headline fit.

\subsection{A 240\,GeV cross-section anchor}\label{sec:indep-anchor}

The steeply-rising threshold makes the $\mW$/$\GammaW$ extraction
\emph{rate-driven}. A precise cross-section measurement far above threshold, where
the line shape is flat, can pin the rate and break the threshold correlations. We
add one point at the $ZH$-run energy $\sqrts=240\GeV$ with its luminosity
$10.8\,\text{ab}^{-1}$~\cite{FCCFSR2025}, predicted by the \emph{same} independent
chain (MoCaNLO NLO-EW $\otimes$ NLL ISR, extended above threshold for this study,
section~\ref{sec:indep-fullrange}), and re-run the 2-POI cov-lumi fit with the
$240\GeV$ point appended as a non-beam-smeared overflow bin
(\code{scripts/investigations/hienergy/dofit\_anchor240.py}).

What the anchor measures depends entirely on the branching-ratio convention, and
the $240\GeV$ response makes it explicit (relative shift of $\sigma(240)$ for
$+50\MeV$ in each POI). In \textbf{pdg-constant}, where the BR is divided out so
$\sigma_\text{obs}\propto\sigma_\text{production}$, the far-above-threshold point
is flat in \emph{both} POIs ($+0.08\%$ in $\mW$, $+0.003\%$ in $\GammaW$). In
\textbf{off-shell}, the native $\sigma(4f)\propto{\rm BR}^2\propto m_W^6/\GammaW^2$
carries the decay branching at \emph{every} energy, so $\sigma(240)$ is a clean
$\GammaW$ handle ($-4.6\%$ in $\GammaW$, the ${\rm BR}^2$ scaling) with negligible
$\mW$ dependence ($+0.46\%$, the residual production part).

\subsection{Principle: what MoCaNLO is missing}\label{sec:match-principle}

The independent MoCaNLO line shape (section~\ref{sec:indep}) and the BFS chain
(section~\ref{sec:val}) are complementary in a way that invites a controlled
combination. MoCaNLO is a \emph{complete, exact} fixed-order NLO-EW calculation of
the full off-shell $e^+e^-\!\to 4f$ process: at $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ it already
contains every threshold effect --- the $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ Coulomb singularity in
the one-loop virtual ($\sim\!\alpha\pi/2\beta$, the
threshold enhancement of~\cite{FKM1993,FKM1995}), the soft and collinear photons,
the non-factorisable production--decay interference, and the $\mathcal O(\alpha)$
electroweak decay correction --- and it computes them \emph{without} the threshold
($v$-)expansion that BFS performs. The BFS chain, by contrast, is a threshold
expansion carried to $\mathcal O(\alpha^2)$: it supplies genuine higher-order pieces
that lie beyond a fixed-order NLO calculation.

This dictates precisely which BFS pieces may be added to MoCaNLO without double
counting. Organising both as a double expansion in the electroweak coupling
$\alpha$ and the threshold velocity $v\simeq\beta$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every \emph{strictly} NLO BFS piece --- the hard matching coefficient
    $c_{p,LR}^{(1,\mathrm{fin})}$, the electroweak decay correction, and the
    $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ Coulomb term --- is $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ and is therefore
    \emph{already inside} MoCaNLO's NLO (and more accurately, being un-expanded).
    Adding any of these would double count;
  \item only the \emph{genuinely higher-order} or \emph{orthogonal} pieces can be
    added: (i) the BFS dominant NNLO threshold block --- the
    $\mathcal O(\alpha^2/v^2)$ Coulomb terms plus the NNLO hard and decay
    corrections~\cite{BFSnnlo} --- which has no overlap with the
    $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ content of MoCaNLO; and (ii) $\delta_\text{QCD}$, the QCD
    correction to hadronic $W$ decay, which is entirely absent from MoCaNLO (its
    matrix element runs at QCD order zero, electroweak only).
\end{itemize}
This is the standard additive-matching prescription used at the top-quark
threshold~\cite{Beneke2015,HoangStahlhofen} --- a complete fixed-order result plus
the higher-order threshold remainder, with the lower-order content of the remainder
subtracted; here the subtraction is automatic, as shown next.

\subsection{The additive construction}\label{sec:match-construction}

The two corrections are applied at the \emph{partonic} level, ahead of a
\emph{single} ISR convolution, so that initial-state radiation dresses the combined
cross section exactly once. Per channel,
\begin{align}
  \hat\sigma_\text{comb}(\hat s) &= \hat\sigma^\text{NLO}_\text{MoCaNLO}(\hat s)
     + \delta_\text{NNLO}(\hat s)\,\hat\sigma^\text{Born}_\text{MoCaNLO}(\hat s),
     \label{eq:match-comb}\\
  \sigma_\text{obs}(s) &= \iint\! D\,D\,\hat\sigma_\text{comb}(x_1 x_2 s),
     \label{eq:match-obs}
\end{align}
with $D$ the (LL or NLL) electron radiator. No $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ subtraction
is applied (section~\ref{sec:indep-calc}, 2026-06-12 correction): both terms of
$\hat\sigma_\text{comb}$ are ISR-naked --- MoCaNLO's $\hat\sigma^\text{NLO}$ is
collinear-counterterm-subtracted, and the BFS-derived
$\delta_\text{NNLO}\hat\sigma^\text{Born}$ never carried ISR --- so the single
radiator convolution dresses the combined cross section exactly once. (The
pre-correction form subtracted $C_1[\hat\sigma^\text{Born}_\text{MoCaNLO}]$
here, which would be correct only for a mass-regularised
$\hat\sigma^\text{NLO}$ carrying the explicit $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ ISR
logarithm.)

The NNLO correction enters as a \emph{relative} production $K$-factor,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_\text{NNLO}(\hat s) = \frac{\sigma^\text{NNLO}_\text{BFS}(\hat s)
     - \sigma^\text{NLO}_\text{BFS}(\hat s)}{\sigma^\text{Born}_\text{BFS}(\hat s)},
  \label{eq:match-dnnlo}
\end{equation}
evaluated from the total $e^+e^-\!\to W^+W^-$ cross section of the BFS chain (BR
stripped, WHIZARD anchor off) with the three terms differing \emph{only} by the
active correction blocks. The relative form is deliberate: any common multiplicative
factor --- the WHIZARD anchor (section~\ref{sec:anchor}), the branching-ratio
convention --- cancels in the ratio, so $\delta_\text{NNLO}$ is a clean,
convention-independent number that transplants onto MoCaNLO's own Born. Because
$\sigma^\text{NNLO}_\text{BFS}-\sigma^\text{NLO}_\text{BFS}$ is, by the additive
structure of the chain (section~\ref{sec:nnlo}), \emph{exactly} the NNLO block --- the
NLO loops appear in both terms and cancel --- $\delta_\text{NNLO}$ contains no
$\mathcal O(\alpha)$ piece. The protection against double-counting MoCaNLO's NLO is
thus structural, not numerical.

\paragraph{Channel scope.} The dominant NNLO terms --- the
$\mathcal O(\alpha^2/v^2)$ Coulomb ladder and the NNLO hard production --- correct the
$e^+e^-\!\to W^+W^-$ amplitude \emph{before} the $W$'s decay and are therefore blind
to the final state: $\delta_\text{NNLO}$ multiplies every WW channel identically. The
single channel-dependent NNLO term is the Coulomb$\times$decay piece; its
leptonic-versus-hadronic spread is
$\text{Coulomb}\times(\delta^\text{EW}_{\text{decay},h}-\delta^\text{EW}_{\text{decay},\ell})
\sim 2\times10^{-5}$, three orders of magnitude below the $0.25\MeV$ target, so the
universal treatment is justified.

\paragraph{$\delta_\text{QCD}$.} The QCD correction to hadronic $W$ decay,
$\delta_\text{QCD}(\alpha_s)=1+\alpha_s/\pi+1.409\,(\alpha_s/\pi)^2\approx1.040$ per
hadronic $W$~\cite{BFSnnlo} (section~\ref{sec:dqcd}), is applied per channel as
$\delta_\text{QCD}^{\,n_\text{had}}$ with $n_\text{had}\in\{0,1,2\}$ the number of
hadronically decaying $W$'s ($\mu\tau$: $1.000$; $\ell\nu qq$: $1.040$; $qqqq$:
$1.082$). Averaged over the WW cross section ($\sigma$-weighted --- the colour factor
$N_c$ per quark line boosts the hadronic share, so the effective
$\langle n_\text{had}\rangle\approx1.36$, not the bare channel-multiplicity $0.8$)
this is a flat $+5.43\%$ on the rate ($\approx\langle n_\text{had}\rangle\times4.0\%$),
entirely on the decay side and
orthogonal to the production correction. Unlike the BFS chain, MoCaNLO has no
reference $\alpha_s$ to divide out, so the \emph{full} factor is applied.
\emph{Relation to the BFS reference.} The two chains measure different final
states, so their $\delta_\text{QCD}$ enters differently. The BFS production fit
(section~\ref{sec:dqcd}) measures the specific semileptonic $\mu\nu qq$ channel
and multiplies it by a single power of $\delta_\text{QCD}$ ($n_\text{had}=1$,
$+4.0\%$). The matched chain here instead fits the inclusive pure-WW rate, so the
\emph{same} per-$W$ factor enters per channel as
$\delta_\text{QCD}^{\,n_\text{had}}$ and accumulates to the $\sigma$-weighted
$+5.43\%$ quoted above; restricted to the semileptonic channel it reduces to the
BFS single power. The per-hadronic-$W$ correction is thus identical between the
two; they differ only through the final state integrated over.

\subsection{NLL ISR via eMELA}\label{sec:match-nll}

The same construction supports an NLL initial-state radiator. The analytic LL$+$exp
radiator of the independent chain (section~\ref{sec:indep-calc}) is replaced, per
leg, by the eMELA NLL electron PDF~\cite{eMELA2019} (DELTA factorisation,
$\alpha(M_Z)$ renormalisation --- the production scheme conventions of
section~\ref{sec:val-isr-scheme}). The port reuses the validated endpoint substitution of
the independent chain: the per-leg $\beta_e$ at $\mu_F=\sqrt{\hat s}$ and the LL
soft$+$virtual normalisation coincide \emph{identically} with those of the BFS-side
eMELA convolution (section~\ref{sec:isr}), so the radiator is exact (verified below).
A historical note: while the observable still carried the $C_1$ subtraction, its
analytic $\beta$-scheme LL form ($\beta=(\alpha/\pi)(2L-1)$, $L=\ln\mu_F/m_e$)
was checked directly against eMELA's own $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ soft behaviour:
in the region where its numerics are valid (omx\,$\approx\!10^{-7}$--$10^{-9}$),
\code{code\_pdf} matches the $\beta$-scheme ($2L-1$) soft form to $0.1\%$ and the
``collinear'' ($2L$, the 1911.12040 convention) form only to $0.3$--$1.1\%$ ---
the soft$+$virtual ``$-1$'' is genuine all-orders soft physics carried by the
resummed ePDF. That result stands as a validation of the eMELA soft endpoint,
although $C_1$ no longer enters the observable. The radiator $\alpha$ conventions:
the LL radiator runs at the grid's EW-scheme $\alpha_{G_\mu}$, and the NLL swap moves to
$\alpha(M_Z)$ in the eMELA \emph{ALPMZ} scheme --- aligned since to the PDG
$1/128.943$ (it formerly used MoCaNLO's internal lepton-PDF $1/128.232$, which was the
source of the apparent NLL-pull instability now resolved in
item~\ref{item:scheme-match}). The measured LL$\to$NLL shift on the matched line
shape at $163\GeV$ is dominated by the $\alpha_{G_\mu}\!\to\alpha(M_Z)$ growth of the
radiator exponent ($\beta$ up ${\approx}3\%$ on a ${\approx}{-}30\%$ damping), with a
small genuine NLL-kernel content on top (the BFS chain, with $\alpha$ held fixed,
measures $+0.28\%$); the $\alpha$-stability scan of item~\ref{item:scheme-match}
isolates these two pieces and shows the kernel part is $\alpha$-stable.

best-motivated central number.

\subsection{Matched Asimov pull}\label{sec:match-pull}

The cross-fit of section~\ref{sec:val-isr-cross} quantifies the impact: inject the
nominal line shape of each matched/NLL variant as Asimov pseudodata and fit it with
the \emph{unmatched-LL} reference morph, so the best-fit shift is the $\mW$/$\GammaW$
bias from omitting that piece (equivalently, the size of the correction the matching
supplies). The self-fit closes at $0.00\MeV$ and the reference sensitivities
reproduce the independent fit of Table~\ref{tab:indep-br}
($\sigma(\mW)=0.52\MeV$ pdg-constant, $1.15\MeV$ off-shell, on the corrected ISR
layer), validating the machinery. Table~\ref{tab:match-pull} gives the
decomposed pulls.

In \emph{shape-only} mode --- which isolates the line-shape bias and is
convention-robust --- the picture is clean:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the BFS \textbf{NNLO} block shifts $\mW$ by $\mathbf{+2.3\MeV}$, identical
    in both BR conventions, landing squarely on the BFS theory-ladder NNLO step of
    $\sim\!2.3\MeV$ (section~\ref{sec:val-theory-ladder}) --- an independent
    confirmation, from a fully different line shape, that the NNLO threshold
    correction matters at the few-MeV level;
  \item swapping the LL radiator for the eMELA \textbf{NLL} one shifts $\mW$ by
    $\mathbf{-1.5\MeV}$ (shape), which an $\alpha$-matched cross-fit
    (\code{scripts/investigations/nll\_isr/crossfit\_nll\_decomp.py}) resolves into
    $-0.6\MeV$ from the radiator-$\alpha$ convention
    ($\alpha_{G_\mu}\!\to\!\alpha(M_Z)$) and $-1.0\MeV$ from the genuine NLL kernel
    in DELTA factorisation --- both small. An earlier concern that this pull was
    \emph{numerically unstable} --- it appears to swing $6.9\MeV$ as $\alpha(M_Z)$
    moves from MoCaNLO's $1/128.232$ to the PDG $1/128.943$ --- has been
    \emph{resolved} (item~\ref{item:scheme-match}, section~\ref{sec:match-todo}). An
    $\alpha$-stability scheme scan shows the swing lives \emph{entirely} in the
    LL $\alpha$-\emph{input} piece --- the reference morph's
    $\alpha_{G_\mu}\!\to\!\alpha(M_Z)$ leading log, which moves $-0.6\MeV\!\to\!-7.4\MeV$
    across that $\alpha$ range --- while the genuine NLL \emph{kernel} pull is
    $\alpha$-\emph{stable} to $0.14\MeV$ over the same range. DELTA is confirmed to be
    the factorisation scheme matched to the Catani--Seymour-subtracted MoCaNLO grid
    (running eMELA in MS$\overline{\text{S}}$ instead over-counts the finite collinear
    term and roughly \emph{doubles} the near-threshold line shape). The $-1.5\MeV$ at
    the production $\alpha(M_Z)=1/128.943$ is therefore a robust line-shape pull; the
    residual sensitivity to $\alpha(M_Z)$ is the physical input uncertainty, carried by
    the $\Delta\alpha$ nuisance, not an unmatched-scheme artifact;
  \item $\delta_\text{QCD}$ is shape-neutral ($0.0\MeV$): a flat $+5.4\%$
    normalisation carries no line-shape information;
  \item the pieces combine almost linearly (matched\,$+$\,NLL: $+0.8\MeV$ shape), and the
    fitted \emph{uncertainty} is unchanged --- the matching moves the central value,
    not the sensitivity.
\end{itemize}

The \emph{production cov-lumi} mode tells the complementary story about the
\emph{rate}: there $\delta_\text{QCD}$'s flat $+5.4\%$ normalisation, omitted from
the templates but constrained by the luminosity covariance, forces a large
compensating shift --- $-53\MeV$ (off-shell) to $-106\MeV$ (pdg-constant) on $\mW$.
This is not a physical ``matching shifts $\mW$ by $100\MeV$'' statement but the
rate-constrained fit's hypersensitivity to the absolute normalisation; it makes
quantitative the requirement that $\delta_\text{QCD}$ --- and the absolute
cross-section normalisation generally --- \emph{must} enter the templates for any
rate-based $\mW$ extraction, which is exactly what the matching provides. The NNLO
shape pull is the convention-robust headline; the cov-lumi normalisation
sensitivity is a caveat on the rate handle, not a property of the NNLO physics.

\subsection{Status of the cross-check items}\label{sec:match-todo}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Matched Asimov pull on the production grid (done).} The pull
    (Table~\ref{tab:match-pull}: $\delta_\text{NNLO}\,{+}2.3\MeV$, NLL ISR
    $-1.5\MeV$ shape-only) has been regenerated on the production
    $m_t=172.5\GeV$ correlated-seed grid; the numbers reproduce the interim
    $174.2\GeV$ grid to $\sim\!0.02\MeV$ (shape) / $\sim\!0.2\MeV$ (cov-lumi
    $\delta_\text{QCD}$), confirming $m_t$-independence.
  \item \textbf{$C_1$ / NLL scheme consistency (resolved).} The question of whether
    $C_1$, kept in its $\beta$-scheme LL form, is consistent with the eMELA NLL DELTA
    radiator has been settled directly (section~\ref{sec:match-nll}): eMELA's soft
    ePDF matches the $\beta$-scheme ($2L-1$) to $0.1\%$ --- not the collinear ($2L$)
    form --- so the soft$+$virtual ``$-1$'' is genuine resummed physics present in both
    and $C_1$ requires no rebuild. The remaining LL$\to$NLL residual is
    $\lesssim0.1\MeV$, as the cross-fit toggle confirmed. Subsequently
    (2026-06-12) $C_1$ was removed from the observable altogether --- the grids
    are collinear-counterterm-subtracted, so there is no $\mathcal O(\alpha)$
    overlap to subtract (section~\ref{sec:indep-calc}); the scheme result above
    stands as an eMELA soft-endpoint validation.
  \item \label{item:scheme-match}\textbf{ISR factorisation-scheme matching (RESOLVED ---
    DELTA is the matched scheme).} The question was whether the eMELA NLL ePDF, convolved
    in the \emph{DELTA} factorisation scheme, is consistent with the MoCaNLO NLO-EW grid,
    whose initial-state collinear singularity is handled by Catani--Seymour dipoles
    (\code{pdf\_set=none}, integrated-dipole \code{idip} run). An $\alpha$-stability
    scheme scan settles it three ways, all confirming DELTA:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Empirical} (\code{scripts/investigations/nll\_isr/scheme\_alpha\_scan.py},
        \code{confirm\_alpha\_decomp.py}). For each scheme, the genuine NLL-kernel pull is
        measured at $\alpha(M_Z)\!=\!1/128.943$ and $1/128.232$. \emph{DELTA} is
        $\alpha$-stable ($-0.98\!\to\!-1.12\MeV$ shape, swing $0.14\MeV$;
        $+1.3\MeV$ cov-lumi, swing $0.06\MeV$); an unmatched $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ term
        scales with $\alpha$ and would show such sensitivity, so its absence proves DELTA
        is matched. \emph{MS}$\overline{\text{S}}$ is catastrophic ($+6.7\MeV$ shape,
        $-384\MeV$ cov-lumi; its convolved line shape is ${\approx}2\times$ DELTA near
        threshold). The apparent $6.9\MeV$ instability of the full pull is the
        \emph{LL $\alpha$-input} piece (the reference morph's
        $\alpha_{G_\mu}\!\to\!\alpha(M_Z)$ leading log, $-0.6\!\to\!-7.4\MeV$), not the
        kernel or the scheme.
      \item \textbf{Literature} (eMELA: BCFS~\cite{eMELA2019}; the $\Delta$-scheme
        definition~\cite{eMELAdelta}). DELTA is the QED DIS-like scheme in which the finite initial-state collinear
        remainder is absorbed into the PDF, so $\hat\sigma=\sigma_\text{Born}$; eMELA's
        \code{MSBAR} is the standard $\overline{\rm MS}$ ($K_{ij}\!\equiv\!0$). The two
        differ by the closed-form $\mathcal O(\alpha)$ term
        $D_{\overline{\rm MS}}\!-\!D_\Delta=-(\alpha/2\pi)\,[(1{+}x^2)/(1{-}x)\,(2\ln(1{-}x){+}1)]_+$,
        and the soft$+$virtual endpoint constant $\lambda_1$ is scheme-\emph{independent}.
      \item \textbf{Numerical closure.} eMELA's measured
        $x\,(D_{\overline{\rm MS}}\!-\!D_\Delta)$ at $Q\!=\!161\GeV$ reproduces that
        analytic term to ${\sim}3\%$ at $x\!=\!0.9$ (correct sign throughout), confirming
        eMELA implements the standard transformation.
    \end{itemize}
    Why DELTA, not $\overline{\rm MS}$: with \code{pdf\_set=none} and massive electrons there
    is no beam PDF to mass-factorise into, so no initial-state collinear counterterm is
    applied (the surfaced \code{idip} is a small $+0.4$--$2.5\%$ of Born, not a large
    collinear subtraction); $\hat\sigma_\text{NLO}$ is the inclusive = DELTA hard
    coefficient, and the energy-degrading ISR collinear log lives in the ePDF. The earlier
    expectation ``CS dipoles $\Rightarrow\overline{\rm MS}$'' conflated IR finiteness with
    mass factorisation. DELTA is therefore already the correct production choice; no scheme
    change is needed.
  \item \textbf{NLL soft-endpoint substitution (resolved).} The eMELA-NLL per-leg
    integrand uses the analytic soft$+$virtual limit below $1-x<10^{-15}$ ($\approx13\%$
    of the quadrature weight). Quantified: as $x\!\to\!1$ the integrand must approach
    that exact constant, and \code{code_pdf} agrees to $\sim0.2\%$ where its numerics
    are valid (omx\,$\approx\!10^{-7}$--$10^{-9}$) but then \emph{diverges} to $+6\%$ at
    omx\,$\approx\!10^{-66}$ (where $x$ underflows to $1.0$). The analytic limit is
    therefore the trusted value and the cutoff \emph{shields} the result from eMELA's
    $x\!\to\!1$ grid-edge artifact; lowering it would \emph{import} a $+0.53\%$ shift
    (shape-only $\Delta\mW=-0.11\MeV$, wrong sign). The cutoff is correct as-is.
  \item \textbf{$\delta_\text{QCD}$ bookkeeping (resolved).} Confirmed: MoCaNLO takes
    the physical (QCD-corrected) $\GammaW$ as an external input while its $W\!\to q\bar
    q$ matrix element runs at QCD order zero, so its hadronic branching ratio
    $\Gamma_\text{had}^\text{EW}/\GammaW^\text{phys}$ is short by exactly
    $\delta_\text{QCD}$ and the per-channel $\delta_\text{QCD}^{\,n_\text{had}}$ factor
    restores it with no residual double-correction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Validation of the WHIZARD-grid morphing scheme}\label{sec:morphval}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

This appendix documents the construction and the validation of the
morphing predictor introduced in section~\ref{sec:morphing}. Every plot
is produced by a script under
\code{scripts/investigations/whizard_grid_highstats/} and refreshed into
\code{report/figs/} by \code{report/publish.sh}.

\subsection{Strategy}\label{sec:morphval-strategy}

The morph turns a discrete WHIZARD grid into a smooth, differentiable
$\sigma(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW)$ usable as the exact-Born anchor. It is
built and validated in well-separated steps, each isolating a single
ingredient so that a failure can be attributed unambiguously:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Input grid.} WHIZARD 3.1.5 four-fermion Born for the
specific channel $e^+e^-\!\to\!\munuqq$ on a rectangular
$(\sqrts,\mW,\GammaW)$ cube: 6363 \texttt{grid\_fine} points
($101\times9\times7$ at 0.1-GeV $\sqrts$ step in $[155, 165]$~GeV) at
$\sim$0.008\,\% Monte-Carlo precision, with the 0.5-GeV outer wings
($154$–$154.5$, $165.5$–$172$~GeV) from the highstats campaign retained
as $\sqrts$-spline support beyond the fine window
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-grid}).
\item \textbf{Per-$\sqrts$ 1D fits.} At every grid $\sqrts$ the $\mW$
and $\GammaW$ dependence is described by independent quadratic LSQ
fits $\mathcal{R}_m$, $\mathcal{R}_\Gamma$, taken at the nominal value
of the other parameter (Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-axisfits}).
\item \textbf{Joint coupling.} A single bilinear scalar $\beta(\sqrts)$
absorbs the $(\mW,\GammaW)$ cross term that the product of the two 1D
morphs cannot reproduce (Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-cross}).
\item \textbf{$\sqrts$ interpolation.} The eight $\sqrts$-dependent
quantities are carried between grid $\sqrts$ by natural cubic splines
(Figs.~\ref{fig:mv-sqrts}, \ref{fig:mv-sqrtsval}, \ref{fig:mv-4dloo}).
\item \textbf{Closure.} The predictor is checked against two
independent held-out WHIZARD campaigns: a 1-MeV $(\mW,\GammaW)$ plane
at three $\sqrts$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-substep}) and sub-MeV
$(\mW,\GammaW)$ 1D scans at $\sim$0.005\,\% MC on the same three
$\sqrts$ slices (Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-validate-fine}), and against the
published BFS WHIZARD 4f Born tables (Fig.~\ref{fig:mv-bfs}).
\item \textbf{Physics sanity.} The morphed line shape and the
$\GammaW$-induced threshold crossing are inspected
(Figs.~\ref{fig:mv-smooth}, \ref{fig:mv-azzurri}).
\end{enumerate}

The predictor is
\begin{equation*}
\sigma_\text{pred}=\sigma_0(\sqrts)\,
\mathcal{R}_m(\sqrts,\mW)\,
\mathcal{R}_\Gamma(\sqrts,\GammaW)\,
\bigl[1+\beta(\sqrts)\,(\mW-\mW^0)(\GammaW-\GammaW^0)\bigr],
\end{equation*}
with the eight $\sqrts$-dependent quantities --- $\sigma_0$, three
coefficients each of $\mathcal{R}_m$ and $\mathcal{R}_\Gamma$, and
$\beta$ --- fitted independently at every grid $\sqrts$ and then
interpolated along $\sqrts$ with a natural cubic spline.

An interpolating spline threads every node, so per-$\sqrts$
Monte-Carlo fluctuations would be propagated as a ripple. To prevent
that the grid is \emph{denoised along $\sqrts$ before the morph fits
it}. The denoising is done on physical, slowly-varying quantities,
never on the morph coefficients: the ratios
$\sigma/\sigma_0$ --- flat in $\sqrts$, since the steep threshold rise
cancels --- are smoothed with a $\chi^2$-targeted penalised spline
(consistent with the MC error bars, not threaded through every point),
and $\sigma_0$ itself, too steep to $\sqrts$-smooth without rounding
the threshold knee, is denoised instead by averaging across the
$(\mW,\GammaW)$ plane ($\approx\!\sqrt{45}$ noise reduction). Smoothing
the three strongly anti-correlated $\mathcal{R}_m$ / $\mathcal{R}_\Gamma$
polynomial coefficients directly was tried and rejected --- it breaks
the cancellation that makes their combination $\mathcal{R}(\cdot)$
accurate and inflates the $\sqrts$ leave-one-out residual roughly
threefold --- which is why the denoising acts on the physical $\sigma$
ratios. Every morph input is then smooth in $\sqrts$ and the cubic
spline threads clean points. With the \texttt{grid\_fine} sampling
(0.1-GeV $\sqrts$, $\sim$0.008\,\% MC) the per-$\sqrts$ noise the
denoising removes is already small, and the procedure is largely
cosmetic; the algorithm is left enabled by default to absorb residual
fluctuations and keep the recipe identical to the half-density-grid
configuration used in earlier validations.
The single scalar $\beta(\sqrts)$ is treated separately: it is not
part of an anti-correlated coefficient group, so a $\chi^2$-targeted
weighted \texttt{UnivariateSpline} (the same recipe used on the
$\sigma/\sigma_0$ ratios) is applied to the per-$\sqrts$ $\beta$
series directly, with weights derived from the per-$\sqrts$ error
$1/\sqrt{\sum_k w_k A_k^2}$ propagated from the bilinear LSQ. The
cubic spline that the morph carries threads the smoothed $\beta$
nodes, dropping the step-to-step ripple by $\approx$10\,\% beyond
the upstream denoising without affecting any held-out closure metric.

\section{$K_C$ decommission and the $K_C$-safe diagnostic alternative}\label{sec:kcsafe}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Decision (2026-05-26).} The production card now sets
\code{NLO_CONFIG["include_coulomb"]=False}: the Fadin--Khoze--Martin
Coulomb resummation factor $K_C$ is no longer multiplied onto the chain.
This appendix documents (i) why the previous $K_C\times\sigma$
combination is incorrect, (ii) what the K$_C$-safe knob
\code{NLO_CONFIG["coulomb_kc_safe"]} would have done as a partial
patch, and (iii) the numerical evidence behind the decommission.
The K$_C$-safe knob is retained as a card-level diagnostic alternative
for anyone wanting to recover the legacy $K_C$-on chain without the
leading-$\alpha/v$ double-count.

The Fadin--Khoze--Martin $K_C$ (\code{coulomb_K_factor}
in \code{eft_xsec.py}; refs:\ Fadin--Khoze--Martin~\cite{FKM1993}
and FKM-Stirling~\cite{FKM1995}) is a LEP-era closed-form
multiplicative Coulomb resummation factor that predates the BFS
unstable-particle EFT formalism by roughly a decade. \emph{Neither BFS
paper uses it}: BFS treat the Coulomb correction via an
$\alpha$-expansion of the all-order Coulomb Green function
$G_C^{(0)}(0,0;E_W)$ (eq.~(61) of \cite{BFSnlo}, eq.~(9) of
\cite{BFSnnlo}); eq.~(62) of \cite{BFSnlo} is its
$\mathcal{O}(\alpha)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ expansion. BFS explicitly
argue \emph{not} to resum the two-photon piece ($\sim$ few permille at
threshold; \cite{BFSnlo} lines 1722--1725) and add the eq.~(62)
contribution as an additive correction. The same Green-function
prescription is used throughout the PNRQCD tt-threshold literature
(Beneke et al.~\cite{Beneke2015}, Hoang--Stahlhofen~\cite{HoangStahlhofen});
no published EFT calculation uses a multiplicative
$K_C\times\sigma$ structure. Our framework's $K_C$ multiplier was a
pragmatic LEP-era engineering shortcut that survived into the BFS
chain assembly and double-counted the leading $\alpha/v$ Coulomb against
the BFS eq.~(62) addition. Dropping it brings the production chain
into the apples-to-paper configuration used in
section~\ref{sec:val-table4} (Scenario~F of \code{validate_bfs_nlo.py}).
A full $G_C$-based refactor (``Grade~B'' in the team memory) would
restore the all-order Coulomb resummation rigorously; until then we
accept BFS's $\sim 0.2\%$ unresummed-two-photon trade-off.

The technical details below are unchanged from the original K$_C$-safe
appendix and describe the partial-patch knob that survives in the
card for diagnostic comparisons.

\subsection*{The leading-$\alpha/v$ overlap (background)}

When $K_C$ is on, the BFS NLO Coulomb correction
$\Delta\sigma_\mathrm{Coul}^{(1)}$ of eq.~(62) of
\cite{BFSnlo} (\code{delta_sigma_Coulomb_NLO_specific_pb}, switched on
together with the rest of the NLO loop chain by
\code{NLO_CONFIG["include_NLO_hard_decay"]=True}) describes overlapping
physics on its leading-$\alpha/v$ piece. The first term of eq.~(62),
\begin{equation}
\Delta\sigma_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(1),\,\mathrm{term\,1}}=
\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{27 s_W^4 s}\,
\mathrm{Im}\!\left[-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\!\frac{-(E+i\GammaW)}{\mW}\right],
\qquad E=\sqrts-2\mW,
\end{equation}
is the unresummed first-order $\pi\alpha/v$ Coulomb correction of
\cite{FKM1995} eq.~(21) ($X/2\simeq +5.21\%$ at threshold), exactly
the leading-$\alpha/v$ piece that $K_C-1$ resums. With the framework's
off-shell-momentum prescription $K_C-1=+7.0\%$ at threshold; the
$\sim 2$\,pp offset to FKM's $X/2$ is the finite-$\GammaW$
regularisation chosen to keep $\sigma$ smooth in $(\mW,\GammaW)$.
Multiplying $K_C$ onto a $\sigma$ that already contains
$\Delta\sigma_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(1),\,\mathrm{term\,1}}$ therefore
counts the leading Coulomb \emph{twice}. The second term of eq.~(62),
\begin{equation}
\Delta\sigma_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(1),\,\mathrm{term\,2}}=
\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{27 s_W^4 s}\,
\mathrm{Im}\!\left[\frac{\alpha^2\pi^2}{12}\sqrt{-\mW/(E+i\GammaW)}\right],
\end{equation}
is the two-photon $\alpha^2$ subleading piece
($X^2/6\simeq +0.17\%$ at threshold). It is not part of $K_C$'s
leading-$\alpha/v$ resummation and remains a genuine NLO correction
when $K_C$ is on.

\paragraph{The K$_C$-safe knob (diagnostic only).} A card knob
\code{NLO_CONFIG["coulomb_kc_safe"]} (default \texttt{False}; only
takes effect when \code{include_coulomb=True}) selects
between two interpretations of the eq.~(62) addition inside
\code{_add_nlo_loops_to_LR}, for users wishing to compare against the
historical $K_C$-on chain without the leading-$\alpha/v$ double-count:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{False} --- legacy $K_C$-on chain. The full eq.~(62)
    (term~1 $+$ term~2) is added on top of $K_C$. The
    leading-$\alpha/v$ Coulomb is double-counted at the $\sim 5\%$
    level on $\sigma$ at threshold. This chain is \emph{not} the
    production default (which has $K_C$ off) and has no direct BFS
    Table-4 cross-check.
  \item \texttt{True} --- $K_C$-safe. Eq.~(62) is added with the
    \code{subleading_only} flag, i.e.\ only term~2 (the
    $\alpha^2$ two-photon $\sim 0.2\%$) is retained. Combined with
    $K_C$, the chain avoids the leading-$\alpha/v$ double-count while
    keeping the genuine non-Coulomb NLO pieces (HSC and decay) and
    the subleading Coulomb correction beyond $K_C$. Still differs
    from the production K$_C$-off chain by the $\sim 2$\,pp off-shell
    prescription residual discussed below.
\end{itemize}
The knob is plumbed end-to-end through \code{sigma_partonic_munuqq}
(\code{eft_xsec.py}), \code{sigma_observed_munuqq} (\code{isr.py}), and
\code{WWGenerator.template_fingerprint} --- the freshness check in
\code{doFit_ww.py} therefore aborts the fit if the templates were
generated under a different setting than the live card. The current
fingerprint captures every chain knob discussed in this report
(\code{NLO\_CONFIG}, the $\alpha_\text{em}$ inputs, all
\code{isr\_emela\_*} scheme choices, the variation grid, BR
constants, and the EW parameter inputs); the residual gap is the ISR
quadrature settings ($z_\text{min}$, $n_\text{quad}$) which are
currently module constants in \code{isr.py} and not exposed in the
card --- if those are tweaked locally for studies, the templates must
be regenerated by hand.

\paragraph{Validation.} Four cross-checks under
\code{scripts/investigations/coulomb_double_count/} pin the
implementation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Route-A vs Route-B identity.} The two-call difference
  $\sigma(\texttt{kcsafe}{=}\mathrm{False}) -
   \sigma(\texttt{kcsafe}{=}\mathrm{True})$ from the chain matches the
  standalone term-1 piece
  $\Delta\sigma_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(1),\,\mathrm{term\,1}}$ assembled
  directly from the helper, with the appropriate $27/4$
  specific-to-total factor (and $\times\delta_\mathrm{QCD}$ when
  active). The maximum relative error is $<10^{-14}$ both with and
  without $\delta_\mathrm{QCD}$ --- i.e.\ machine precision; the
  individual per-row values are floating-point round-off and vary at the
  last digit from run to run.
\item \textbf{Same-physics overlap.} $K_C-1$ and
  $\Delta\sigma_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(1),\,\mathrm{term\,1}}/\sigma_{LR}^{(0)}$
  are both $\sim 5{-}7\%$ at threshold, fall to $\sim 3\%$ at
  $\sqrts=172\GeV$, and track each other with a near-constant
  $\sim {+}2$\,pp offset --- two formulations of the same physics.
\item \textbf{FKM 1995 strict near-threshold.} At
  $\sqrts=2\,M_W^{\mathrm{BFS\,ref}}=160.754\GeV$, the standalone
  term-1 piece evaluates to $+5.27\%$ on $\sigma_{LR}^{(0)}$ vs FKM's
  $X/2=+5.21\%$ --- agreement at the $1\%$ relative level.
\item \textbf{Legacy-chain magnitude.} Relative to the legacy
  $K_C$-on chain (PDG-constant BR, full NLO$+$NNLO,
  $\delta_\mathrm{QCD}$, Whizard anchor), the $K_C$-safe combination
  reduces $\sigma_\mathrm{partonic}$ by $-7.9\%$ at $\sqrts=157\GeV$,
  $-7.6\%$ at $161\GeV$, $-6.5\%$ at $163\GeV$. The shift is smooth in
  $\sqrts$ with no new kinks;
  $\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\mW$ and $\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\GammaW$
  retain the same overall shape (Fig.~\ref{fig:kcsafe-chain}). The
  production K$_C$-off default sits a further $\sim 2$\,pp below the
  K$_C$-safe curve at threshold (see Fig.~\ref{fig:kcsafe-overlap}
  and the structural discussion above).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Asimov closure is preserved by both chains. Dropping $K_C$ shifts
$\Delta\mW$ by $+0.04\MeV$ on the default FCC-ee scenario (well
sub-MeV) and by $0\MeV$ on the Azzurri scenario; $\rho(\mW,\GammaW)$
shifts by $+0.02$ on the default scenario, by $0$ on the Azzurri
scenario. The luminosity nuisance fully absorbs the constant
$\Delta\sigma/\sigma \sim 5{-}8\%$ between the two chains across the
scan; the residual $\sim 0.3\%$ shape slope is too small to register
at the precision of these Asimov fits. The $\mW$ central value
recovered from the pseudodata is identical to 6-digit precision in
all four runs.

\paragraph{Verdict.} Grade~A is safe to ship: dropping $K_C$
preserves Asimov closure, leaves the m\textsubscript{W} central value
unchanged, and changes the statistical uncertainty by less than
$0.05\MeV$. The Grade~B G$_C$-based refactor (project memory) is a
nice-to-have publication upgrade rather than a bug fix, and can be
scheduled behind the parallel-implementation cross-check.

\paragraph{What the morphing scheme sees.} The WHIZARD Born anchor
$f(\delta,\GammaW)$ of section~\ref{sec:anchor} and
Appendix~\ref{sec:morphval} is a Born-level matching factor
$\sigma_\mathrm{WHIZARD}/\sigma_\mathrm{BFS-EFT,\,Born}$; it does not
depend on $K_C$, NLO loops, or the \code{coulomb_kc_safe} setting,
so dropping $K_C$ from the chain does not require any morph
regeneration.
